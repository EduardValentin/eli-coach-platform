# Design System

## Theme

Evoa has a warm, premium, and modern visual identity. Soft off-white surfaces, restrained borders and shadows, and generous whitespace keep the product calm and approachable. Magenta is the primary brand and action color; teal provides a grounded counterpoint for secondary actions and recovery-oriented content. Elegant serif headings add personality, while a clean sans-serif keeps the interface practical and easy to scan.

The design balances human coaching with professional competence. Photography and small product-UI compositions may add warmth and context, but the interface itself remains clean, focused, and consistent across the public, client, and coach surfaces.

## Audience

The product is designed for women seeking personalized strength and nutrition coaching, including women with or without an active menstrual cycle. Its warm palette, editorial headings, supportive copy hierarchy, and body-aware domain colors make the experience feel personal without relying on stereotypically feminine decoration. Clear controls, calm surfaces, and predictable structure support clients during workouts as well as the coach managing detailed plans.

## Design System

The production source of truth is `packages/ui/src/styles.css` for tokens and, for components, `packages/ui`'s eleven concern subpaths (`appointments`, `calendar`, `filters`, `layout`, `lib`, `motion`, `overlays`, `portal`, `primitives`, `tabs`, `toast`), imported one at a time — the package has no root barrel. The reference app mirrors the same visual roles and may keep prototype-only tokens and composed components where the production surface does not exist yet.

Every button shares one base: the 8px `field` corner, a 2-unit gap, no wrapping, 50% opacity when disabled, and the one focus rule below. A size sets the height, a horizontal padding that narrows beside a leading or trailing icon (`px-3` for `xs`, `px-4` for `sm` and `lg-tight`, `px-6` for `md`, `px-8` for `lg`) and a text size; `lg-tight` is for a full button that shares its row with another action. A `full` button fills its row; `full-below-sm` fills it only below the `sm` breakpoint, where stacked actions take the whole width. `raised` is the only elevation: the `action` shadow lifting to `action-hover` on hover. `glass` is the one translucent tone for an action over the hero; the header's portal link uses it while the header is transparent and `primary` once it turns solid. Production's `Button` is the union of the reference app's `ui/button` and `ThemeButton`, without `link`: the portal sizes come from the former, the public sizes from the latter. In the portals a button's size follows where it lives, `xs` (32px) in a table or list row, `sm` (40px) inside a widget, card or dialog footer, `md` (48px) for page header actions and form submits, and its variant follows its importance: at most one `primary` per group and always last, `outline` for alternatives, `ghost` for dismiss and icon-only controls, `destructive-outline` for a loss action in a row and solid `destructive` only inside a confirm dialog. Every size carries 14px medium text except `md` at 16px. Navigation inside a widget is a text link, never a button. Its shadcn `Tabs` carries a `segmented` variant — the view switch of the coach's assessment calls page and both check-in pages — which wraps to a second row rather than overflowing its container, so its triggers grow from their own width instead of sharing a zero basis; below `sm` a list of four or more triggers becomes a two-column grid, and a last trigger left alone on its row spans both columns rather than sitting half width. One rule keeps the filter controls apart: a page takes at most one segmented control, the view switch over mutually exclusive slices, and every further narrowing takes a `sm` `Select` whose options each carry a `count` badge. A segmented trigger carries a `count` badge only for the one slice holding items that wait on the viewer, the coach's Pending and the client's Requests. A view switch nested under the page's segmented control, such as the food library's catalog, tag board and swap groups inside the nutrition hub's Foods tab, takes single-choice filter chips rather than a second segmented control. Five pages follow it: the coach's assessment calls page, both check-in pages and both resources pages. The assessment calls page is where both meet. Its `When` control heads the panel's left column with the `Status` `Select` under it, and the `sm` (40px) search field heads the right column with the sort control under it: the two columns sit side by side from `sm` and stack, `When` first, below it, and the search label is spoken rather than drawn. The sort control is a `sm` `Select` over Scheduled date, Booking date, Name and Email, named `Sort by` by its `aria-label` with no visible caption and showing the key with its order, beside an `outline` `icon-sm` direction toggle, `aria-pressed` once the order leaves the key's default and named for the order it shows ("Soonest first", "Newest first", "A to Z" and their reverses). `SortControl` is one component: each page hands it its own `SortOption`s — a key, its label, whether it orders chronologically or alphabetically (which picks the direction glyph), its default direction and the names of both directions — and a `size`, `sm` (40px) by default or `md` (48px) for touch layouts. The coach's resources page carries a `Tag` `Select` whose options carry counts on the left and the search field and the sort control (Date added, Title) on the right; the client's resources page carries the tag `Select` and the search only. Below `md` the tag filter and the search share one row, the coach's sort under them, every control at 48px for touch, and the toolbar is absent while the list is empty. Every row of the coach's clients list wears one badge from a single client status vocabulary — Paid, Invited, Onboarding, Awaiting review, In review, Needs details, Approved, Active, Cancelled, Inactive — neutral while she is still arriving, `pending` where the coach owes her an answer, teal while the review runs, `success` once the coaching is running and muted once it is cancelled or over; the coach's onboarding widget wears the same badge. Its shadcn `Badge` carries a `brand-secondary` tone for the coach's "Today" call badge, on the assessment calls page and on the dashboard's upcoming-calls widget: teal ink and a teal outline on `brand-secondary-surface`, because the tint alone does not separate the pill from the surfaces it sits on. A second tone, `muted` — muted ink inside the standard border and no fill — is the quiet status tag that stands in for the join action on a call that has ended. A third, `pending`, carries `status-pending` ink on `status-pending-soft`: the waiting start path beside a client's name. A fourth, `success`, carries `success` ink on `success-soft`: a client whose coaching is running, on the clients list. An onboarding answer the coach must look at twice — a safety `Yes`, pregnancy or postpartum — takes no badge at all: `ReviewAnswerValue` prints it as plain text and adds one small `destructive` warning glyph after it, in the coach's onboarding widget, reading and reviewing alike. Its shadcn `Select` item takes `surface-muted` on hover, on `data-[highlighted]`, and on focus, because a select opened inside a dialog cannot rely on focus alone; `SelectContent` also runs its own 800 ms typeahead over the `data-typeahead` tokens an item declares, so the phone dialling codes answer to an ISO code and to the code's digits where Radix matches only one `textValue` prefix. Its shadcn `Dialog` content carries `size`: `default` (`sm:max-w-lg`), `md` (`sm:max-w-2xl`, the width every `ResponsiveSheetDialog` takes rather than setting one through `contentClassName`), `wide` (a `sm:max-w-3xl` frame up to 90vh whose header, body and footer parts take their own padding, for the photo set dialog), `screen` (the whole viewport inside the safe-area insets, no corner or border, for the progress-photo lightbox) and `viewer` (the `screen` frame below `lg`; from `lg` a centred frame on the `card` corner, `max-w-6xl` wide and 90dvh tall capped at 56rem, for the resource viewer). `screen` and `viewer` draw their own close control. Its shadcn `Accordion` serves the coach's onboarding widget, whose forms open one by one, and the collapsible widget frame: `border-subtle` rules between items, a `text-secondary` chevron, and brand ink on hover instead of an underline. An Accordion trigger renders its own heading around its button, an `h3` by default and an `h2` when it titles a widget, where its `title` layout centres the chevron on the title line and drops the section layout's vertical padding. The coach reviews her answers in a dialog opened from that widget: every item opens, each answer row takes a check box at its leading edge, and a bar sticky to the bottom of the dialog carries the flagged count, the note and the review actions — approving her answers through the same confirmation as the widget's own approve button, asking for more details, and leaving the review. Its shadcn `RadioGroup` item is the check box's round twin — 20px on the base surface with the soft shadow, taking the brand border and a brand dot when chosen — so the onboarding's yes/no questions carry the same weight as its check boxes. Its shadcn `Pagination` has its first consumer on the assessment calls page, and its numbered links and previous and next controls sit on the `xs` step of the control scale. `PaginationLink` also takes `asChild`, and the previous and next controls place their chevron and label around a `Slottable`, so a caller can hand in a router `Link` without restating the primitive's own content.

The reference app also has reusable product compositions of its own: `ToggleChip` for multi-select pills built on the `Toggle` button primitive, `CheckboxChip` for a multi-select fieldset that must read as a checkbox group to assistive technology (same pill look and soft brand-tinted selected state as `ToggleChip`, but backed by a visually hidden native checkbox rather than a button), `ResponsiveSheetDialog`, which presents the same content in a mobile bottom sheet or desktop dialog and owns the sheet form's frame through its parts — `SheetDialogHeader` (a hairline-ruled band with an optional caller eyebrow, the title on the `lg`/`xl` semibold rung and an optional one-line description), `SheetDialogBody` (the scrolling body) and `SheetDialogActions` (the action group, stacked full width with the primary on top below `sm` and in a row with the primary last from `sm`); the measurements sheet, the review-call scheduler and the resource form all use them, `DeadEndContent`, the dead-end body — an icon medallion, an optional muted eyebrow, one `<h1>`, body copy, and at most one action supplied by the caller — `ErrorPage`, the full-page `main` that wraps it behind the 404, the 403, the failed-sign-in page and the "call link not ready" page a join link renders while the coach has not saved a meeting room, and two tiers of coach appointment. The coach portal's `AssessmentCallsUnavailable` composes the same body inside a portal panel rather than a page, because the portal shell already owns `main`. `DeadEndPanel` takes one optional caller action; `ResourcesUnavailable` uses it for a `md` outline "Try again" and stands in for the page header, so the panel's `<h1>` stays the page's only one. `AppointmentCard` carries the full record, on the check-in lists and the assessment calls page: an attendee's photo or initial, their name in a caller-chosen element, a date and time row, optional mail and tel links on one row, an optional two-column definition list of caller-supplied details (the assessment calls page lists age, gender, goal and country), and caller-supplied badges, quote, footnote and actions; its `past` status drops the shadow and mutes the name and the initial, and below `md` its actions stack under the content at full width. `DashboardAppointmentRow` carries the summary, and is the one row both dashboard cards use — an attendee's name, one muted date and time line, optional badges and a single action. A surface showing an appointment in full takes the card; a dashboard card summarising several takes the row. Its local `PhoneFrame` and `SectionEyebrow` mirror the production components. `ZoomableImage` is the swipe and double-tap zoom stage shared by the progress-photo lightbox and the resource viewer. Both portals show a client's resources through one `ResourceCard` and one `ResourceViewer`. Only PDFs and images have a page preview; Word and Excel files are download-only and show `ResourceFileCover` instead, the one cover both components share: the quiet surface with the kind's glyph (`FileText` for Word, `FileSpreadsheet` for Excel) centred in `icon-muted` at a light stroke, 56px on phones and 64px from `sm` in a card, and in the viewer 64px, 80px from `lg`, with the original file name under it on the body rung in secondary ink. The card is a single button on the card surface: a 3:4 thumbnail of the first page, or the cover, on the `field` corner with a subtle border, anchored to the top; a row with the `muted` file-type badge (PDF, DOC, XLS, IMG), the page count when a PDF has more than one page and, for the client, a `pending` "New" badge until she opens it; the title on the body rung in medium, clamped to two lines; and up to two `brand-secondary` tag badges then "+n". The coach's ghost overflow menu (Edit details, Delete) sits beside that row as a sibling of the button, never inside it. The grid takes two columns on phones, three from `md` and four from `xl`. The viewer pairs the pages with the details: below `lg` a full-screen sheet with a back control and the title on top, the page stage with swipe and a "2 / 8" counter between ghost previous and next buttons, then the description, the tags and a definition list (Type, Pages, Size, Added) scrolling under it, and Download as a full-width `md` primary in a bar pinned to the bottom; from `lg` the stage fills the left of the `viewer` dialog and a bordered details column holds the same content with Download as a full-width `sm` primary at its foot. The coach's outline Edit details and `destructive-outline` Delete sit at the end of the details. A download-only resource puts the cover on the stage, without swipe, zoom, counter or arrows, on a phone stage 224px tall rather than the page stage's up to 58% of the viewport so the details and Download sit higher, and its definition list has no Pages; images have no Pages either. A one-page resource shows no counter and no arrows; arrow keys turn pages, each turn is announced politely, and each page image is named "{title}, page n". Its `MetricTile` carries a toned icon and four tones where the production component has two and tones the label instead; the two have not been reconciled.

Keyboard focus is drawn by one unlayered `:focus-visible` rule in `theme.css` rather than per component: the `focus-visible:ring-*` and `focus-visible:outline-*` utilities the primitives carry paint nothing in this app, and the primitives also carry `outline-none`, which as a utility beats anything in `@layer base`. The indicator is a 2px `--focus-ring` outline at 2px offset with a soft 16% halo, matching the brand ring production already draws. `--focus-ring` resolves to `--brand`, which clears 5.5:1 on white and 4.9:1 on the warm page background, well over the 3:1 SC 1.4.11 asks of a non-text indicator; controls inside an element marked `data-surface="inverted"` (the hero, and the header while it is transparent over it) switch to the lighter `--brand-on-inverted`, because the brand pink only reaches 2.7:1 against near-black. Menu and option items, and `tabindex="-1"` skip-link targets, are excluded because they suppress their outline deliberately and signal focus another way; a base rule also removes the browser outline those targets would get on programmatic focus. A sibling rule matches `label[data-chip-control]:has(:focus-visible)` with the same outline and halo: a chip whose real control is a visually hidden native input (`CheckboxChip`, and the file inputs behind `FileDropzone` and `FilePickerButton`) cannot show its own outline, since an `opacity: 0` element's outline is invisible too, so the attribute hands the same indicator to the visible label instead. `data-chip-control` is the opt-in for that case; any future chip built the same way must carry it. `TagInput` draws the indicator the same way on its field: the bare text entry carries `data-field-entry`, which the global rule skips, and the frame around the chips carries `data-field-frame`, which takes the outline and halo while the entry has keyboard focus.

Every form built on `Input`, `TextArea` and `TagInput` — the booking details, the cart, the coach tools — draws one field from the shared `fieldSurfaceClass` recipe in `ui/input.tsx` (with `fieldStateClass` for focus and invalid states on a native control): 48px tall, the 8px `field` corner, the base surface as its fill so it reads as a well on the page and matches the card it sits in, the soft control border, and the border taking the interaction colour (`focus-ring`, the brand colour on the public site and near-black in the coach portal) on focus. Callers set only layout (width, an icon inset, a textarea height), never the look. The waitlist capture keeps its own pill field on its dark and light surfaces. Portal pages that still draw raw underlined inputs have not been moved onto `Input` yet. No field sets its own focus colour — the one rule above owns that.

Both portals own their page frame once. `CoachLayout` and `PortalLayout` draw the same content column — `max-w-portal`, centred, with a 16px gutter at phone width, 24px from `sm` and 32px from `lg`, and the matching vertical rhythm — so no routed page sets its own outer padding, bottom padding or centring. A page adds only a reading width where a form needs one. The client portal's shell has the coach's anatomy: on large screens a fixed sidebar with her name block, an icon medallion beside her full name, above the navigation; below `lg`, a 64px top bar carrying the same name block and a bottom tab bar of at most four tabs plus More, whose `BottomSheet` holds her name block, the links the bar has no room for and Sign out. The medallion is its own role rather than an `Avatar`: a round `User` glyph in `primary` ink on `primary-soft`, 40px in the sidebar and the More sheet and 36px in the top bar, standing in until a client picture exists. The top bar pads its top, and the tab bar and the sheet their bottom, by the device's safe-area insets. The installed app's name and colours belong to production. Every routed page opens with `PortalPageHeader`, which owns the one `<h1>` and the 32px gap to the content below it; a page with a richer hero, such as the coach's client detail, keeps its own layout but takes the title's typography from `PORTAL_PAGE_TITLE_CLASS`. The builder canvases — the training, nutrition-plan, nutrition-day and recipe builders — keep their own sticky chrome bar in place of a page header, because their toolbar is the page's heading row and has to stay with the canvas as it scrolls.

Public-site compositions use narrowly scoped `public-*` radius, size, and shadow tokens rather than adding raw repeated values.

### Typography

The portals set type on a five-rung ladder: the page title in the serif face at regular weight; the hero value (`value-lg`, 24/32 sans medium with tight tracking and tabular numerals); the widget title (16px semibold, the only 600 weight inside a widget); body (14px regular, with medium for the one emphasized token in a row); and the label (`label`, 12px semibold uppercase at `tracking-label`) for eyebrows, table heads and definition labels. Bold is reserved for the 10px `micro` marks. The serif face appears only on the page title, the brand word and a widget's voice line.

Inside the portals, interaction colour is `primary` and its derivatives, never `brand`: `primary` is the brand magenta on the client portal and near-black on the coach portal, and the focus ring follows it, so checked controls, active navigation, hovered triggers and keyboard focus agree on each side. `brand` is kept for the public site and brand identity marks. Attention markers such as unread dots and counters are `status-pending`. `AttentionDot` is the one 8px dot: the notification bell's unread marker and each unread notification, and the client navigation, which marks a link in the sidebar, the More sheet and on the More tab when the link lives in the sheet, while it holds something she has not opened, and speaks it as "(new)" after the link's name. Teal is shared by both portals and does not separate them.

A stat widget has one anatomy: a header of icon and title, where the title is the hero's label so no uppercase label sits under it; the hero, with an optional inline unit in 14px medium secondary on the same baseline; one 14px secondary context line directly under the hero; and a footer pinned to the card's bottom that holds navigation only. Management actions live in the header as ghost icon buttons with a spoken label, one per action and a menu only for two or more, and the client portal shows the same widget without them. An empty state keeps the card's shape: a muted placeholder in the hero slot, the explanation in the context line, and the one thing to do as a `sm` primary text button in the body. A widget in a narrow column, such as the client sidebar's next check-in, takes a compact density that scales the same anatomy down one step: a 14px semibold title with its icon, body-size content, 16px padding on the card corner with no shadow, one `sm` primary button at full width, and the navigation link centred beneath it as part of the same stacked group. A list widget that the viewer may add to, such as the client's measurements, carries a quiet add in the same header slot, a `sm` outline "Add" with a leading plus, since the header never holds a filled control; while the list is empty the header add gives way to a `sm` primary text button in the body that names what is added ("Add your first measurements"). A page has at most one primary action, so the profile's picture controls are small outline and ghost buttons beside the avatar, and the identity block sets the name on the hero rung with the email as its context line rather than under an eyebrow. Secondary detail at the bottom of a page, such as the booking behind the coach's Assessment call widget, takes the collapsible widget frame: the same panel and header of icon and title, where the `h2` title is an Accordion trigger spanning the header with the chevron at its trailing edge, collapsed by default, and the body opens 16px under the header.

A settings section is a panel with a header band carrying an icon and the widget-title rung plus an optional one-line description, hairline-separated rows each with a title, a short description and an example and no icon of their own, since the header's icon names the section, the control trailing the text for an instant preference or stacked under it for a form field; preferences apply on change, a form saves once from a footer button disabled until something changed, warnings read inline in the pending tone, and no page has a page-wide save. A settings page holds settings sections only, never a dashboard widget: the client's subscription is a section whose rows are the plan, whose description carries the payment date and whether it has started, started, or been cancelled, so it wears no status badge, the renewal or access date, and, while cancelling is possible, a cancel row whose description states the terms (the refund deadline, or that access stays to the period end with no refund) beside a red-ink outline button that opens the confirm dialog; the coach's client record keeps the read-only subscription summary widget.

An appointment is one card on both portals, the other party is its attendee (the client on the coach's pages, the coach on the client's), the type and the status are badges from the shared vocabulary, and the actions sit in the card's action slot, trailing on wide screens and stacked below on phones. The card's date sits on the body rung beside the name, and a join link on a list card is an outline button, since the page's one primary is the request action; only the sidebar's next check-in, whose sole purpose is joining, carries a solid join. In a dashboard list every row action is an outline, and at most one row per widget, the one that is live right now such as a call marked Today, carries the solid join. A check-in's status is always a badge beside its type, pending in colour when the viewer owes the reply and muted when the other party does, and the action slot holds buttons or nothing; both portals' check-in pages share the frame of segmented tabs, a muted count line, and the card list at the portal column's full width.

- `DM Sans` is the body and interface family; `Playfair Display` is the heading and display family. Both are emitted at runtime as `--font-sans` and `--font-serif`, which code outside CSS (the generated resource page art) reads instead of naming a family.
- The core scale is the framework's `xs` to `5xl` steps plus `caption` (11px) for the smallest readable text — calendar weekdays, chart axes, phone-preview body copy — `md` (15px) between `sm` and `base`, `label` (12px semibold at `tracking-label`, the uppercase store tags and badges), and the display steps `display-sm` (24px), `display-md` (32px), and fluid `display-lg` (44–72px).
- Available weights are regular 400, medium 500, and semibold 600. Two more are reserved: light 300 for the hero's lead copy over video, and bold 700 for prices and the tracked uppercase `micro` labels. Line heights are the framework's `tight` (1.25, every heading that sets one), `snug`, `normal` and `relaxed`, plus `display-snug` (1.1) for large display headings and `heading` (1.2) inside the display steps.
- `tracking-label` and `tracking-section-eyebrow` are the letter-spacing roles; everything else uses the framework's `tracking-*` steps at their own values, `tracking-wide` (0.025em) for navigation links and short badges.
- `micro` (10px) is the one step below the core scale: the count on an icon control such as the cart button, tracked uppercase overlines and badges, and phone-preview actions.
- `public-my-method-figure-heading`, `public-cycle-phase`, `public-footer-cta-*`, and `phone-*` typography tokens are intentionally scoped to compact public-site compositions and phone previews.

### Spacing

The spacing scale is `space-0` (0), `space-1` (4px), `space-2` (8px), `space-3` (10px), `space-4` (12px), `space-5` (16px), `space-6` (24px), `space-7` (32px), `space-8` (48px), `space-9` (64px), and `space-10` (96px).

- `space-1` to `space-2` handle icon gaps and other micro-spacing.
- `space-3` to `space-5` handle compact control padding and tightly related content.
- `space-6` to `space-7` handle card padding and component groups.
- `space-8` to `space-10` handle major layout and section separation.
