# Architecture

Last audit: 2026-09-15 at commit 148d594f, scope apps/platform, packages, tools, knip.json, eslint.config.mjs. Last update: 2026-09-28 at commit f9ecc1e5, change review. Maintained by the architecture inspection workflow; changes ship with the PR that causes them.

## Shape

The business rules live in `packages/domain` (C1), published as eighteen subpaths with no root barrel: one folder per entity (`account`, `acquisition`, `assessment-call`, `cart`, `client`, `client-invitation`, `client-journey`, `coach-availability`, `coach-meeting-room`, `coaching-bundle`, `coaching-subscription`, `download-grant`, `email-address`, `feature-flag`, `payment-link`, `product`, `waitlist`) plus the interface-only `shared`. Rules are methods on the entity classes that carry the decisions — account lifecycle and portal access (`Account#isActive`), waitlist availability and reduced-pricing allocation, delivery limits and their windows, download-grant validity and delivery shape, publication slug, taxonomy, size-cap, digest and target rules, cart reconciliation, purchasability (`Product.evaluatePurchasability`), file-format resolution, email normalization (`EmailAddress`), and the assessment-call rules — which starts in the coach's own zone are open (`CoachAvailability.openSlotStarts`, `isOpenStart`, stepping in minutes from the window's opening, where a start is open only when the call ends inside the window and the call and its buffer overlap none of the coach's busy intervals), whether a slot may be reserved (`AssessmentCall.decideReservation`, where a taken slot is refused whoever holds it), and the assessment call's slot policy (`ASSESSMENT_CALL_RULES`, a `SlotPolicy` the `/assessment-call` slice owns and hands to `CoachAvailability`: the duration, read once to derive `endsAt` on the snapshot so no adapter re-applies it, the buffer, which with the duration sizes the coach's time a call reserves, and the step, horizon and lead time; `SlotPolicy.of` rejects a policy whose step is shorter than the call or whose values are not whole minutes) — and the coaching-sales rules — a payment link's thirty-day validity and usability (`PaymentLink.isUsable`), the bundle catalog's per-tier prices (`CoachingBundle`), the fourteen-day withdrawal deadline (`withdrawalDeadline`), a subscription born from a completed checkout (`CoachingSubscription.fromCompletedCheckout`), and a paid client's invitation — pending until used or thirty days old, accepted once and only by the subject that first accepted it (`ClientInvitation.isPending`, `wasAcceptedBy`) — and journey (`ClientJourney.step`, `welcomeWording`) — and `<Verb><Noun>UseCase` classes, each with one `execute` method, sequence them. Forty-nine published ports and one unpublished one are named for their consumers' capabilities: eighteen gateways (`Accounts`, `WaitlistEntries`, `StoreCatalog`, `StoreAcquisitions`, `StoreProductPublications`, `DownloadGrants`, `FeatureFlags`, `ProductAssets`, `AssessmentCallReservations`, `CoachAvailabilitySource`, `CoachCalendar`, `PricingEligibility`, `CheckoutSessions`, `CoachingPurchases`, `PaymentCheckout`, `PaymentLinks`, `AssessmentCallReader`, `CallSalesStates`, `ClientInvitations`, `InvitedClients`, `ClientJourneys`, `IdentityInvitations`), the five notification ports (`WaitlistConfirmation`, `ProductDelivery`, `AssessmentCallNotifications`, `CoachingSalesNotifications`, `ClientInvitationNotifications`), the two consumer ports one slice's use case asks another slice's work through, satisfied by a composition (`InvitationAcceptance`, `PaidClientAdmission`), the writer, digest, hasher and token ports (`ProductAssetWriter`, `ProductAssetDigest`, `PayloadDigestGenerator`, `DownloadTokenHasher`, `PaymentLinkTokenGenerator`, `PaymentLinkTokenHasher`, `InvitationTokenGenerator`, `InvitationTokenHasher`, `ClientInvitationIdGenerator`), the two `/coach-availability` and `/coach-meeting-room` change ports the coach's own settings save through (`CoachAvailabilityChanges`, `CoachMeetingRoomChanges`) and the read port `CoachMeetingRoomSource`, the input boundary `FeatureFlagReader`, `Clock` as the sole `shared` port, and `AcquisitionIncidents`, `WaitlistIncidents`, `AssessmentCallIncidents`, `CoachingSalesIncidents` and `ClientInvitationIncidents` beside the use cases that consume them. `DownloadTokenGenerator` remains deliberately unpublished because its only consumer is inside the package. Waitlist mode is persisted policy input, not deployment configuration: `Waitlist` is the configured waiting-list aggregate whose cap and active offer are shared by availability and registration, and `GetWaitlistUseCase` reads the existing `FeatureFlagReader` on every execution, reads `WAITLIST_MODE` from the published `FeatureFlagSet` (true enables, false or absent disables), and assembles the plain `WaitlistSnapshot` from that mode, the entity's offer and its computed availability. A failed flag read is reported through `WaitlistIncidents.waitlistModeReadFailed()` and safe-enables the waitlist with availability unknown. `AssessmentCallBookingWindow` reads the same flag, through the same key, for booking, and `CoachingSalesWindow` for coaching sales: each is open only while the flag is not true, and closed on a failed read, reported through its slice's incidents port. The resulting `waitlist → feature-flag`, `assessment-call → feature-flag` and `payment-link → feature-flag` edges enter the sibling's index, stay in the use-case ring and close no cycle. The offer object is readonly but not deeply immutable; that R17 cost is accepted until offer mutation or a second offer model is required. The package declares no dependencies and its tsconfig sets `"types": []`, so neither an import nor an ambient Node global reaches inside it — which is why the wall-clock arithmetic the assessment-call slots need is written against `Intl.DateTimeFormat` alone, in `coach-availability/zoned-time.ts`, rather than against a date library.

Details plug in through those ports. Each feature under `apps/platform/src/features/<feature>/` holds its Postgres repositories (`data/`), email adapters (`email/`), HTTP controllers and route modules (`api/`), route fragment (`routes.ts`), wire schemas, path literals and anything else both halves must word identically (`contracts/`, the one browser-safe folder the server half and the browser half may both import), request-context key and guards (`server/guards/`), its composition (`server/<feature>-composition.server.ts`) and its browser code (`ui/<slice>/`, with presenters and step machines in `ui/shared/` and `ui/public/`). `packages/infrastructure` (C6) holds adapters and factories — Turnstile bot detection, Resend and in-memory product email, bearer-secret management auth, the feature-flag repository, the coach calendar (the coach's own availability window, read and saved, alongside the Postgres read of her reserved time and the transaction-scoped writers every appointment kind reserves through), the coach's meeting room (read and saved), the payment provider (Stripe and in-memory checkout and webhook adapters, and the shared `payment_events` ledger every purpose records its events in from inside its own transaction), the identity provider's invitations (Clerk and in-memory, behind C1's `IdentityInvitations`), HTTP helpers and PWA definitions — and owns the six adapter-facing contracts `BotVerifier`, `ProductEmail`, `ManagementAuthenticator`, `PaymentEvents`, `PaymentCompletionHandler` and `PaymentWebhookIncidents` inside their matching concerns. `packages/test-support` (C16) is a dev-only fixture package no production package declares. `packages/db`, `packages/config`, `packages/content` and `packages/ui` are leaf packages; `packages/ui` publishes eleven concern subpaths and the stylesheet with no root barrel; `calendar` is where a composite over a third-party widget goes so that consumers of `primitives` do not carry the vendor, and `toast` holds the second such composite the same way (`Toaster` over `sonner`), its own subpath rather than a place inside `overlays` beside `Sheet` so that `overlays`' other consumers do not carry `sonner`. The application composition root is `apps/platform/src/server/container.server.ts`, memoised behind `getPlatformContainer()` and imported only by `root.server.ts`: it builds the shared handles once, including one `createConsoleLogger()` that implements the five domain incident interfaces and C6's `PaymentWebhookIncidents`, the payments checkout and event verifier, the identity invitations, and one `PostgresFeatureFlagRepository` behind one stateless `GetFeatureFlagsUseCase`, selects by the C3 `FEATURE_FLAG_OVERRIDES` concern (`browser` by default when `ENVIRONMENT` is `local` or `test`, `none` elsewhere, `browser` refused when `ENVIRONMENT=production`) whether that reader is handed on raw or wrapped by the `server/feature-flag-overrides/` decorator, which overlays the request's `ff.<FLAG>` query and `__eli_feature_flags` cookie overrides that the override middleware parses and holds in an `AsyncLocalStorage` store around `next()`, and calls the five feature compositions (`accounts`, `assessment-calls`, `coaching-sales`, `store`, `waitlist`), all synchronous, handing the same feature-flag reader to the platform, waitlist, assessment-calls and coaching-sales compositions; a composition that hands a port to another returns `{ feature, handles }` (waitlist → `pricingEligibility`, assessment calls → `assessmentCallReader`, coaching sales → `paymentCompletionHandler`, which the platform's Stripe webhook dispatches to by checkout purpose, and `invitationAcceptance`, which accounts' provisioning asks), the container passes those handles in dependency order, and only `feature` reaches a request context; `platform-composition.server.ts` constructs no persistence. The platform feature-flag API, the public-site shell and the booking window each execute that reader per request, with no result cache and no production write path; in `browser` mode the same parameterless `execute()` answers with the request's overrides on top and the database row is never written. Only `container.server.ts` imports `server/feature-flag-overrides/`; `root.server.ts` mounts its middleware between Clerk and the feature contexts. Routes and loaders read their feature's request-context key (`accountsContext`, `sessionContext`, `storeContext`, `waitlistContext`, `assessmentCallsContext`, `coachingSalesContext`, `platformContext`, `runtimeConfigContext`) off `args.context` instead of resolving anything from a container. Surfaces (`public-site`, `client-portal`, `coach-portal`) assemble features into products and own only route modules, shells and sections; the client portal wraps every client route in a pathless access layout that runs the accounts role guard and the coaching-sales journey gate; `public-site` reaches the booking feature through a route fragment and one path literal, and the coaching-sales feature through two route fragments, its context key, the bundle selector its pricing page renders, the invitation states its invitation page renders, and the journey guard and vocabulary its nav's portal link is resolved from; at runtime its layout also reads the route handles of the booking page and of its own pricing page, which both ask for a full-bleed content frame. `coach-portal` owns the assessment-calls page, which composes the assessment-calls feature's section with the coaching-sales feature's sales blocks, because the coaching-sales feature is downstream of assessment calls and neither imports the other's UI.

Boundaries are enforced by three tools. `tools/dependency-cruiser.config.cjs` declares 38 forbidden rules — package export maps and dependency-absence for the workspace packages, and named rules for every app-level boundary (feature internals, surface-to-feature and surface-to-surface, composition root, browser half, route thinness, guards, domain slices, infrastructure subpaths, the feature-flag override folder's single importer, the assessment-calls feature never reaching the coaching-sales feature, Clerk confined to the accounts feature, the root and the identity adapter, UI layering, workspace-by-name, stability and five hygiene rules) — `knip.json` holds published surfaces at zero unused exports, files, dependencies and unlisted dependencies, and `tools/domain-layout.mjs` checks the domain package's per-entity folder layout. All three run in `pnpm check:boundaries`, directly inside `pnpm validate` and the Docker builder stage, so a crossing fails workspace validation and image builds. `tools/boundaries.test.mjs` proves the dependency-cruiser rules against `tools/boundary-fixtures/`: 37 of the 38 rules have a fixture, and `stability` has none because `moreUnstable` needs dependent counts a fixture tree cannot express — the real-tree cruise is its only proof. `tools/domain-layout.test.mjs` exercises `tools/domain-layout.mjs` against `tools/domain-layout-fixtures/` and against the real domain package; all six of the tool's checks have a failing fixture. Two rules stay review-owned by decision: controller shape (no base controller, no per-request instance field) and failure mapping (an adapter never swallows an infrastructure failure into a result-union member; a named expected condition may be classified and everything else is rethrown). The classified conditions are ENOENT in the filesystem asset store; Postgres `22P02` in the assessment-call repository (a malformed booking id is a mistyped URL) and Postgres `23P01` on the `coach_time_reservations_no_overlap` exclusion constraint in C6's `reserveCoachTime` (losing a race for the coach's time is a taken slot), both read through C2's `isCausedByDatabaseError`; the waitlist signup's unique and check violations on its three named constraints, which register at the regular price or refresh consent; Postgres `23505` on `clients_assessment_call_id_unique` in the coaching purchases repository (a second payment for one call) and on `clients_auth_subject_id_unique` in the client invitations repository (a lost acceptance race); and, in the Stripe adapters, a missing checkout session, an already-closed session on expiry, and an unverifiable webhook signature or body. Each is recorded in `decisions.md`. The component graph has no cycles at module, component, domain-slice or UI-subpath level. Deployment adds artifact controls outside the dependency rules: the platform package's `files` allowlist emits only `build`, the config, content, domain, infrastructure and UI packlists keep production source and artifacts while excluding colocated test/spec files, and the Docker builder asserts that the deployed artifact contains `build/server/index.js`, has no top-level `src` or `e2e`, has no test/spec file under `node_modules/@eli-coach-platform`, and omits the dev-only test-support package. Environment, Docker, CI, integration and Lighthouse wiring no longer carry `WAITLIST_MODE`; migration 0018 seeds the persisted flag true, and the Playwright suite leaves it alone, taking bookings out of waiting-list mode per browser through the public `ff.WAITLIST_MODE=false` override.

## Reading this folder

- `components.md`: the enforced units and what each publishes.
- `units.md`: classes, modules, functions, interfaces, and data structures, with their kind and ring.
- `dependencies.md`: every source dependency, the forbidden edges, the ports, entry points, and shared data shapes.
- `metrics.md`: stability and abstractness per component.
- `decisions.md`: deferred decisions, cohesion groups, cohesion position per component, intended exceptions, open questions.
- `conventions.md`: where a file goes and what it may import; the folder layout the dependency rules assume.

Terms: a *unit* is a class, module, function, interface, or data structure. A *component* is a group of units behind one published interface that tooling enforces. An *edge* from A to B means A's source code names B. *Policy* is business rules; *detail* is databases, frameworks, transports, vendors, UI, and the data shapes they produce. A *ring* is a band of units at one level of distance from inputs and outputs; dependencies point inward.
