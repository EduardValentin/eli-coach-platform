# Architecture

## Shape

The business rules live in `packages/domain` (C1), published as twenty-three subpaths with no root barrel: one folder per entity (`account`, `acquisition`, `assessment-call`, `cart`, `client`, `client-invitation`, `client-journey`, `client-onboarding`, `client-profile`, `client-roster`, `coach-availability`, `coach-meeting-room`, `coaching-bundle`, `coaching-subscription`, `download-grant`, `email-address`, `feature-flag`, `measurement`, `payment-link`, `product`, `unit-preference`, `waitlist`) plus the interface-only `shared`. Rules are methods on the entity classes that carry the decisions — account lifecycle and portal access (`Account#isActive`), waitlist availability and reduced-pricing allocation, delivery limits and their windows, download-grant validity and delivery shape, publication slug, taxonomy, size-cap, digest and target rules, cart reconciliation, purchasability (`Product.evaluatePurchasability`), file-format resolution, email normalization and the subaddress rule (`EmailAddress`, `isAcceptedBy` an `EmailSubaddressPolicy` the container selects: refused only when `ENVIRONMENT` is `production`, and checked first by the booking, waitlist and store use cases), and the assessment-call rules — which starts in the coach's own zone are open (`CoachAvailability.openSlotStarts`, `isOpenStart`, stepping in minutes from the window's opening, where a start is open only when the call ends inside the window and the call and its buffer overlap none of the coach's busy intervals), whether a slot may be reserved (`AssessmentCall.decideReservation`, where a taken slot is refused whoever holds it), and the assessment call's slot policy (`ASSESSMENT_CALL_RULES`, a `SlotPolicy` the `/assessment-call` slice owns and hands to `CoachAvailability`: the duration, read once to derive `endsAt` on the snapshot so no adapter re-applies it, the buffer, which with the duration sizes the coach's time a call reserves, and the step, horizon and lead time; `SlotPolicy.of` rejects a policy whose step is shorter than the call or whose values are not whole minutes) — and the coaching-sales rules — a payment link's thirty-day validity and usability (`PaymentLink.isUsable`), the bundle catalog's per-tier prices (`CoachingBundle`), the fourteen-day withdrawal deadline (`withdrawalDeadline`), the subscription a completed checkout buys (`PurchasedSubscription.fromCompletedCheckout`), and the subscription's lifecycle (`CoachingSubscription`): the day a waiting client's work starts (`programWorkStart`) and the one window in which she may let the coach start now (`startNowUntil`, `startNow`), which cancellation applies (`cancellationRule`: a full refund only for a waiting start before the withdrawal deadline, otherwise a cancellation with access until the paid-through date, nothing once cancelled or ended), the cancellation itself and the instruction it gives the payment provider (`cancel`), the refund due and its settlement (`RefundDue`, outstanding until the provider's refunds reach the amount owed; `hasRefundOutstanding`), the payment problem (`hasPaymentProblem`), the card on file (`PaymentCard`, whose detach clears only the stored card), the derived status and portal access (`statusOf`, `hasPortalAccessAt`: a cancelled subscription reads ended once her access end has passed), whether it is cancelled or ended (`isCancelledOrEnded`), and the mirror of the provider's subscription, invoice and refund events — and a paid client's invitation — pending until used or thirty days old, accepted once and only by the subject that first accepted it, reissued with a fresh token on resend, and standing `pending`, `expired` or `email-failed` for the coach (`ClientInvitation.isPending`, `wasAcceptedBy`, `standing`) — and journey (`ClientJourney.step`: `welcome` until the welcome is seen, `onboarding` until submission, then `submitted`, `in-review`, `needs-details` or `approved` by `/client-onboarding`'s `reviewStageOf` over the four review stamps; `welcomeWording`, five-part when her forms include cycle context) — and the client status the coach's roster shows (`CLIENT_STATUSES`: `invited` until an account is bound while her subscription is not cancelled or ended, else the subscription's derived status, else the journey step's) and whether a refund is due (`hasRefundOutstanding`) — and the onboarding rules: the questionnaire and who is asked what (`ONBOARDING_FORMS`, `formsForGender`, the reveal, conceal and requires rules and exclusive options), a draft that keeps only asked answers, a submission refused once submitted, without the special-category and disclaimer consents, or with an invalid answer, carrying the first measurement entry and the profile facts (`ClientOnboarding.submit`, `profileFactsOf`), PAR-Q screening skipped outside ages 15–69 and its outcome, nutrition-advice, pregnancy and migraine-contraception flags and cycle mode for the coach's review, and the review itself — opened once, approved only while awaiting or in review, a detail request only in review with a note and questions she was asked, answered only on the asked questions and validated in her units (`OnboardingReview`, `DetailRequest`) — and the unit preference (`UnitPreference`, metric or imperial, and the kg↔lb and cm↔in conversions and entry steps in `measure-units.ts`), the measurement fields and their validation (`MEASUREMENT_FIELDS`: weight 30–300 kg and waist 40–200 cm required, hips, thigh and arm optional; `measurementProblem` words a missing or out-of-range reading in the reader's units; `measurementEntryOf` makes no entry without weight and waist), a client's measurement history and when she is due (`MeasurementHistory.dueLine`: measurements 28 days after the latest full set, a weigh-in 7 days after the latest entry), read for her together with what is due now and her units, metric until she chooses (`ReadOwnMeasurementHistoryUseCase`), the waist-to-height ratio of an entry (`waistToHeightRatio` in `/measurement`), progress photos (`ProgressPhoto.accepts`: JPEG, PNG or WebP of at most 10 MiB; front, side and back views; the coach may open every photo, a client only her own, and only the owner removes one) and the photo consent (`ClientProfile.hasPhotoConsent`, recorded once, without which a photo is refused) — and `<Verb><Noun>UseCase` classes, each with one `execute` method, sequence them. `StoreProductPublications`, `DownloadGrants`, `FeatureFlags`, `ProductAssets`, `AssessmentCallReservations`, `CoachAvailabilitySource`, `CoachCalendar`, `PricingEligibility`, `CheckoutSessions`, `ClientInvitations`, `InvitedClients`, `ClientJourneys`, `IdentityInvitations`, `ClientIdentities`, `ClientRoster`, `ClientOnboardingSource`, `OnboardingClients`, `OnboardingReviews`, `ClientMeasurementsSource`, `ClientMeasurementRecords`, `ClientProfiles`, `MeasurementClients`, `ProgressPhotos`, `ProgressPhotoStore`, `ClientUnitPreferencesSource`, `UnitPreferenceClients`), the seven notification ports (`WaitlistConfirmation`, `ProductDelivery`, `AssessmentCallNotifications`, `CoachingSalesNotifications`, `ClientInvitationNotifications`, `OnboardingDetailsNotifications`, `RefundNotifications`), the two consumer ports one slice's use case asks another slice's work through, satisfied by a composition (`InvitationAcceptance`, `PaidClientAdmission`), the writer, digest, hasher, token and id ports (`ProductAssetWriter`, `ProductAssetDigest`, `PayloadDigestGenerator`, `DownloadTokenHasher`, `PaymentLinkTokenGenerator`, `PaymentLinkTokenHasher`, `InvitationTokenGenerator`, `InvitationTokenHasher`, `ClientInvitationIdGenerator`, `DetailRequestIdGenerator`, `ProgressPhotoIdGenerator`), the six change ports a save goes through (`CoachAvailabilityChanges`, `CoachMeetingRoomChanges`, `ClientOnboardingChanges`, `ClientUnitPreferences`, and the journey's `OnboardingSubmissionStamps` and `OnboardingReviewStamps`), the read port `CoachMeetingRoomSource`, the image port `ProgressPhotoRenditions`, the input boundary `FeatureFlagReader`, `Clock` as the sole `shared` port, and nine incidents ports beside the use cases that consume them (`AcquisitionIncidents`, `WaitlistIncidents`, `AssessmentCallIncidents`, `CoachingSalesIncidents`, `CoachingSubscriptionIncidents`, `ClientInvitationIncidents`, `ClientOnboardingIncidents`, `MeasurementIncidents`, `ClientRosterIncidents`). `DownloadTokenGenerator` remains deliberately unpublished because its only consumer is inside the package. Waitlist mode is persisted policy input, not deployment configuration: `Waitlist` is the configured waiting-list aggregate whose cap and active offer are shared by availability and registration, and `GetWaitlistUseCase` reads the existing `FeatureFlagReader` on every execution, reads `WAITLIST_MODE` from the published `FeatureFlagSet` (true enables, false or absent disables), and assembles the plain `WaitlistSnapshot` from that mode, the entity's offer and its computed availability. A failed flag read is reported through `WaitlistIncidents.waitlistModeReadFailed()` and safe-enables the waitlist with availability unknown. `AssessmentCallBookingWindow` reads the same flag, through the same key, for booking, and `CoachingSalesWindow` for coaching sales: each is open only while the flag is not true, and closed on a failed read, reported through its slice's incidents port. The resulting `waitlist → feature-flag`, `assessment-call → feature-flag` and `payment-link → feature-flag` edges enter the sibling's index, stay in the use-case ring and close no cycle. The offer object is readonly but not deeply immutable; that R17 cost is accepted until offer mutation or a second offer model is required. The package declares no dependencies and its tsconfig sets `"types": []`, so neither an import nor an ambient Node global reaches inside it — which is why the wall-clock arithmetic the assessment-call slots need is written against `Intl.DateTimeFormat` alone, in `coach-availability/zoned-time.ts`, rather than against a date library, and why the photo ports carry bytes as `Uint8Array`.

Details plug in through those ports. Each feature under `apps/platform/src/features/<feature>/` holds its Postgres repositories (`data/`), email adapters (`email/`), HTTP controllers and route modules (`api/`), route fragment (`routes.ts`), wire schemas, path literals and anything else both halves must word identically (`contracts/`, the one browser-safe folder the server half and the browser half may both import), request-context key and guards (`server/guards/`), its composition (`server/<feature>-composition.server.ts`) and its browser code (`ui/<slice>/`, with presenters and step machines in `ui/shared/` and `ui/public/`), whose user-action writes go through React Router fetchers against the feature's own resource routes, and every resource route a fetcher writes to recovers a failed write in a one-line `clientAction`. `packages/infrastructure` (C6) holds adapters and factories — Turnstile bot detection, Resend and in-memory product email, bearer-secret management auth, the feature-flag repository, the coach calendar (the coach's own availability window, read and saved, alongside the Postgres read of her reserved time and the transaction-scoped writers every appointment kind reserves through), the coach's meeting room (read and saved), the payment provider (Stripe and in-memory checkout, subscription, customer-card and webhook adapters, the provider-neutral subscription-change, refund and card-change shapes with the coaching purpose's mapper, and the shared `payment_events` ledger every purpose records its events in from inside its own transaction), the identity provider's invitations (Clerk and in-memory, behind C1's `IdentityInvitations`; a replacement revokes the earlier invitation before it creates the fresh one), the client's progress photos (an AES-256-GCM encrypted filesystem store and an in-memory double behind C1's `ProgressPhotoStore`, selected by `CLIENT_MEDIA_PROVIDER`), their renditions (`sharp`, behind C1's `ProgressPhotoRenditions`, in the only module allowed to name it), HTTP helpers (the server's request and error helpers under `./http/server`, and under the browser-safe `./http` the `fetcherOutcomeOf` every fetcher-written resource route's `clientAction` returns) and PWA definitions — and owns the nine adapter-facing contracts `BotVerifier`, `ProductEmail`, `ManagementAuthenticator`, `PaymentEvents`, `PaymentCompletionHandler`, `PaymentSubscriptionChangeHandler`, `PaymentRefundHandler`, `PaymentCardHandler` and `PaymentWebhookIncidents` inside their matching concerns. `packages/test-support` (C16) is a dev-only fixture package no production package declares. `packages/db`, `packages/config`, `packages/content` and `packages/ui` are leaf packages; `packages/ui` publishes eleven concern subpaths and the stylesheet with no root barrel; `calendar` is where a composite over a third-party widget goes so that consumers of `primitives` do not carry the vendor, and `toast` holds the second such composite the same way (`Toaster` over `sonner`), its own subpath rather than a place inside `overlays` beside `Sheet` so that `overlays`' other consumers do not carry `sonner`; the one dialog frame `overlays`' `Dialog` and `layout`'s `ResponsiveSheetDialog` both render is package-private in `lib`, the one subpath both may import. The application composition root is `apps/platform/src/server/container.server.ts`, memoised behind `getPlatformContainer()` and imported only by `root.server.ts`: it builds the shared handles once, including one `createConsoleLogger()` that implements the nine domain incident interfaces and C6's `PaymentWebhookIncidents`, the payments checkout, subscriptions, customer cards and event verifier, the identity invitations, the progress-photo store and renditions, and one `PostgresFeatureFlagRepository` behind one stateless `GetFeatureFlagsUseCase`, selects by the C3 `FEATURE_FLAG_OVERRIDES` concern (`browser` by default when `ENVIRONMENT` is `local` or `test`, `none` elsewhere, `browser` refused when `ENVIRONMENT=production`) whether that reader is handed on raw or wrapped by the `server/feature-flag-overrides/` decorator, which overlays the request's `ff.<FLAG>` query and `__eli_feature_flags` cookie overrides that the override middleware parses and holds in an `AsyncLocalStorage` store around `next()`, and calls the seven feature compositions (`waitlist`, `assessment-calls`, `coaching-sales`, `client-profile`, `client-onboarding`, `accounts`, `store`), all synchronous, handing the same feature-flag reader to the platform, waitlist, assessment-calls and coaching-sales compositions; a composition that hands a port, a handler or a transaction-scoped writer to another returns `{ feature, handles }` (waitlist → `pricingEligibility`; assessment calls → `assessmentCallReader`; coaching sales → `paymentCompletionHandler` and `subscriptionChangeHandler`, which the platform's Stripe webhook dispatches to by purpose, `refundHandler` and `paymentCardHandler`, the one handler it hands every refund and every card change, `invitationAcceptance`, which accounts' provisioning asks, `clientIdentities`, `measurementClients` and `unitPreferenceClients` for client profile, and `onboardingClients`, `onboardingReviewStamps`, `onboardingSubmissionStamps` and `reviewStampWriter` for client onboarding; client profile → `measurements`, `unitPreferences`, `recordMeasurementEntry` and `saveClientProfile` for client onboarding), the container passes those handles in dependency order (waitlist, assessment calls, coaching sales, client profile, client onboarding, platform, then accounts and store), and only `feature` reaches a request context; `platform-composition.server.ts` constructs no persistence. The platform feature-flag API, the public-site shell and the booking window each execute that reader per request, with no result cache and no production write path; in `browser` mode the same parameterless `execute()` answers with the request's overrides on top and the database row is never written. Only `container.server.ts` imports `server/feature-flag-overrides/`; `root.server.ts` mounts its middleware between Clerk and the feature contexts. Routes and loaders read their feature's request-context key (`accountsContext`, `sessionContext`, `storeContext`, `waitlistContext`, `assessmentCallsContext`, `coachingSalesContext`, `clientOnboardingContext`, `clientProfileContext`, `platformContext`, `runtimeConfigContext`) off `args.context` instead of resolving anything from a container; coaching sales' journey guard also sets `clientJourneyContext`, the signed-in client's journey snapshot, which only its own client-journey controller reads. Surfaces (`public-site`, `client-portal`, `coach-portal`) assemble features into products and own only route modules, shells and sections; the client portal wraps every client route in a pathless access layout that runs the accounts role guard and the coaching-sales journey gate, which reads her journey step and whether her portal access has ended and then admits her to the ended page only, and inside it a sidebar shell, with Dashboard, Profile and Settings in its sidebar, Dashboard and Profile in its tab bar and the client's name as a link to her profile, holds the dashboard, the client-profile feature's profile page and the coaching-sales feature's settings page, while the coaching-sales welcome and ended pages and the client-onboarding page sit outside the shell; its dashboard composes the coaching-sales program status, the client-onboarding open request and the client-profile measurements nudge; `public-site` reaches the booking feature through a route fragment and one path literal, and the coaching-sales feature through two route fragments, its context key, the bundle selector its pricing page renders, the invitation states its invitation page renders, and the portal-standing guard and portal link its nav's portal link is resolved from; at runtime its layout also reads the route handles of the booking page and of its own pricing page, which both ask for a full-bleed content frame. `coach-portal` owns the assessment-calls page, which composes the assessment-calls feature's section with the coaching-sales feature's sales blocks, because the coaching-sales feature is downstream of assessment calls and neither imports the other's UI, and the client page, which composes the coaching-sales, client-onboarding and client-profile features' coach blocks (among them the client-profile feature's shared measurements table and photo dialog); the coaching-sales feature's clients page imports only its own feature and so lives in that feature.

Boundaries are enforced by three tools. `tools/dependency-cruiser.config.cjs` declares 42 forbidden rules — package export maps and dependency-absence for the workspace packages, and named rules for every app-level boundary (feature internals, surface-to-feature and surface-to-surface, composition root, browser half, route thinness, guards, domain slices, infrastructure subpaths, the feature-flag override folder's single importer, four feature-direction rules — the assessment-calls feature never reaching the coaching-sales feature, the coaching-sales feature never reaching the client-onboarding or the client-profile feature, and the client-profile feature never reaching the client-onboarding feature — Clerk confined to the accounts feature, the root and the identity adapter, `sharp` confined to the infrastructure images concern, UI layering, workspace-by-name, stability and five hygiene rules) — `knip.json` holds published surfaces at zero unused exports, files, dependencies and unlisted dependencies, and `tools/domain-layout.mjs` checks the domain package's per-entity folder layout. All three run in `pnpm check:boundaries`, directly inside `pnpm validate` and the Docker builder stage, so a crossing fails workspace validation and image builds. `tools/boundaries.test.mjs` proves the dependency-cruiser rules against `tools/boundary-fixtures/`: 41 of the 42 rules have a fixture, and `stability` has none because `moreUnstable` needs dependent counts a fixture tree cannot express — the real-tree cruise is its only proof. `tools/domain-layout.test.mjs` exercises `tools/domain-layout.mjs` against `tools/domain-layout-fixtures/` and against the real domain package; all six of the tool's checks have a failing fixture. Two rules stay review-owned by decision: controller shape (no base controller, no per-request instance field) and failure mapping (an adapter never swallows an infrastructure failure into a result-union member; a named expected condition may be classified and everything else is rethrown). The classified conditions are ENOENT in the filesystem asset store; ENOENT in the encrypted progress-photo store (a missing photo opens as nothing and deletes as a no-op) and EEXIST on a folder it creates; Postgres `22P02` in the assessment-call repository (a malformed booking id is a mistyped URL) and Postgres `23P01` on the `coach_time_reservations_no_overlap` exclusion constraint in C6's `reserveCoachTime` (losing a race for the coach's time is a taken slot), both read through C2's `isCausedByDatabaseError`; the waitlist signup's unique and check violations on its three named constraints, which register at the regular price or refresh consent; Postgres `23505` on `clients_assessment_call_id_unique` in the coaching purchases repository (a second payment for one call) and on `clients_auth_subject_id_unique` in the client invitations repository (a lost acceptance race); Postgres `23505` on `client_onboarding_submissions_client_id_unique` in the client onboardings repository (a second submission is `already-submitted`) and on the partial `client_onboarding_detail_requests_open_per_client_unique` in the onboarding reviews repository (a request while one is open answers `not-in-review`); in the Clerk identity adapter, a revoke answered 404, or 400 with `invitation_revoked`, `invitation_already_revoked` or `invitation_cannot_be_revoked_code` (the earlier invitation is already settled, so the replacement goes on); in the Stripe adapters, a missing checkout session, an already-closed session on expiry, and an unverifiable webhook signature or body; and, in the progress-photo renditions adapter, any throw from `sharp` while reading or re-encoding an upload, answered `refused` like a format outside JPEG, PNG and WebP. Each is recorded in `decisions.md`. The component graph has no cycles at module, component, domain-slice or UI-subpath level. Deployment adds artifact controls outside the dependency rules: the platform package's `files` allowlist emits only `build`, the config, content, domain, infrastructure and UI packlists keep production source and artifacts while excluding colocated test/spec files, and the Docker builder asserts that the deployed artifact contains `build/server/index.js`, has no top-level `src` or `e2e`, has no test/spec file under `node_modules/@eli-coach-platform`, and omits the dev-only test-support package. `tools/check-deploy-resolves.mjs`, proven by `tools/check-deploy-resolves.test.mjs`, resolves every bare import of `build/server/index.js` from the `pnpm --prod deploy` layout, in the Docker builder and in `pnpm check:deploy`, the last step of `pnpm validate`; because the server bundle inlines the workspace packages, a vendor their adapters import (`@clerk/backend`, `stripe`, `sharp`) is also declared by the platform package. Environment, Docker, CI, integration and Lighthouse wiring no longer carry `WAITLIST_MODE`; migration 0018 seeds the persisted flag true, and the Playwright suite leaves it alone, taking bookings out of waiting-list mode per browser through the public `ff.WAITLIST_MODE=false` override.

## Reading this folder

- `components.md`: the enforced units and what each publishes.
- `units.md`: classes, modules, functions, interfaces, and data structures, with their kind and ring.
- `dependencies.md`: every source dependency, the forbidden edges, the ports, entry points, and shared data shapes.
- `metrics.md`: stability and abstractness per component.
- `decisions.md`: deferred decisions, cohesion groups, cohesion position per component, intended exceptions, open questions.
- `conventions.md`: where a file goes and what it may import; the folder layout the dependency rules assume.

Terms: a *unit* is a class, module, function, interface, or data structure. A *component* is a group of units behind one published interface that tooling enforces. An *edge* from A to B means A's source code names B. *Policy* is business rules; *detail* is databases, frameworks, transports, vendors, UI, and the data shapes they produce. A *ring* is a band of units at one level of distance from inputs and outputs; dependencies point inward.
