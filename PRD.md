# Product Requirements Document

## Product Summary

Evoa is a premium, women-focused fitness and nutrition platform for a personal trainer who offers 1-on-1 online coaching, tailored workout plans, menstrual-cycle-aware nutrition guidance, educational content, and digital products. It supports public discovery and private coaching workflows. The experience feels warm, premium, elegant, and modern while staying accessible.

This document is the source of product behavior, business rules, and vocabulary. It is decomposed into epics and user stories in Linear, which owns delivery scope and sequencing.

## Goals

- Convert visitors into booked assessment calls through a high-quality landing page.
- Support invite-only onboarding into paid 1-on-1 coaching.
- Give the coach operational tools to manage clients, create exercises, build multi-week plans, assign plans, and communicate with clients.
- Give clients a clear, supportive portal to follow their assigned plan and adjust scheduling within allowed constraints.
- Sell free and paid digital products through a digital store.
- Maintain a premium, human brand voice across the experience.

## Non-Goals

- Automatic training prescription. Nothing reaches a client that the coach could not see and override: the builder suggests defaults and each week's targets (Business Rule 34), and the coach stays in control of exercise prescription, progression, and deloads; the builder never adds a Deload block for her.
- Admin roles beyond the coach, community or social features, and advanced analytics.
- Video calling inside the product. Assessment calls and check-ins meet in an external meeting room on Google Meet; the product only links to it.

## Users

**Visitor.** A woman discovering the coach through the public site. She can browse the landing page, blog, pricing, and store, acquire store products with her email, and book a free assessment call with the coach. She cannot create an account. She can see 1-on-1 coaching bundles but cannot check out for coaching without a payment link.

**Client.** An invited, paying woman with an active coaching subscription. In the MVP she uses the client portal to submit her onboarding, manage check-ins, read her training program day by day and download it as a PDF, and maintain her profile and subscription. Post-MVP adds workout logging, the meal plan inside her program, cycle tracking, and in-app messaging. Clients with a regular cycle and clients without an active cycle (amenorrhea, post-menopause, hormonal contraception) receive the same level of personalized coaching.

**Coach.** The trainer running the business. In the MVP she uses the coach portal to manage assessment calls, onboard clients, manage check-ins, review client profiles, build her exercise, food and recipe libraries, and build, version and publish each client's training program. Post-MVP adds the meal plan inside a program, in-app messaging, and workout review.

## Delivery Stages

The MVP includes every requirement in this document except the capabilities explicitly marked Post-MVP. Check-ins, including client and coach scheduling, approval, rescheduling, and Google Meet links, remain in the MVP. The coach's exercise library and the training programs that draw from it are in the MVP, and so are her food and recipe libraries, ahead of the meal plans that will draw from them.

Post-MVP adds three capability groups:

- Training programs: workout logging, workout history and review, and program templates (program building, versions, client delivery and the PDF are in the MVP)
- Nutritional programs: the meal plan inside a program version, built from the food and recipe libraries, and the client's view of it.
- In-app messaging: coach-client conversations and chat-specific system events. Check-in notifications and check-in notes remain available in the MVP without chat.

The reference prototype defaults to MVP mode. Its Dev Toggle can switch to Post-MVP mode, which renders the complete MVP plus these three capability groups. Routes may remain directly reachable because the prototype models scope rather than production authorization.

## Product and Brand Principles

The product consistently feels premium, elegant, warm, supportive, women-focused, competent, trustworthy, clean, modern, and accessible.

Brand voice is personal, human, empowering, supportive, and confident. It avoids generic AI-sounding phrases such as "unlock your potential", "world-class", "seamless experience", and "transformative", emoji-heavy copy, and excessive exclamation marks. Visual identity is documented in `DESIGN.md`.

## Reference Prototype

The product is modelled first in a reference prototype application before it is built in production. The prototype mocks every backend, auth, email, and payment dependency and carries a global Dev Toggle for switching between MVP and Post-MVP scope and between app states such as signed out, client, coach, bundle purchased, waiting list mode, and client needs onboarding. MVP is the default; Post-MVP is a strict superset. Those mocks and the Dev Toggle are prototype conventions, not product requirements. Production uses real authentication, persistence, and email. URLs in this document are production URLs.

# Business Rules

## Access and Roles

1. **Accounts exist only by invitation.** Nobody can sign up on her own. The coach's account is provisioned by the operator; a client's account is created from the invitation her payment triggers. Sign-in uses an email one-time code; creating an account from an invitation needs no code.
2. **Client accounts are invite-only.** A completed payment creates the client and sends her invitation. The invitation is valid for 30 days and can be used once. An expired, used, or unknown invitation link shows the same unavailable page, whichever the reason. Until the client's account exists, the coach can re-send the invitation from her client detail page; each re-send replaces the earlier link, which stops working at once, and restarts the 30 days. A re-send whose email cannot be sent is reported to the coach so she can send it again.
3. **Client portal access requires invitation, a coaching subscription that has not ended, and submitted onboarding.** A client signing in before her onboarding is submitted is held on the welcome screen or her onboarding and cannot reach the rest of the portal until she submits it. A client whose coaching subscription has ended reaches only the page of Business Rule 67.
4. **Coach portal access is restricted to the coach role.** Signed-in accounts without the required role see a clear denied-access page.
5. **Every account has exactly one role, client or coach.** There is no account without a portal.

## Coaching Sales

A **payment link** is the unique link, sent by email after an assessment call, through which the visitor chooses a coaching bundle and pays for it. Her **price tier** is regular or reduced and is resolved from the call's email: reduced when that email holds a reduced-price waitlist allocation (Business Rule 17) in any campaign, regular otherwise. Her **start choice** decides when her program starts: immediately, or after the 14-day withdrawal period. A **coaching subscription** records what she paid for: the coaching bundle, price tier, amount, start choice, and whether it has started. Every assessment call that has ended has a **sales state**: held (no working payment link), payment link sent (a working payment link, not yet paid), or paid (the call's payment created a client). Every client has one **client status**: Invited (she has paid and has no account yet), Onboarding (her account exists and her onboarding is not yet submitted), Awaiting review (her onboarding is submitted and the coach has not opened it), In review (the coach has opened it, or the client has answered a detail request), Needs details (a detail request is open), Approved (the coach has approved her answers), Cancelled (her coaching subscription is cancelled and has not ended), or Inactive (her coaching subscription has ended); once her account exists, Cancelled and Inactive take the place of her onboarding statuses. Active is reserved for when her program has started. A **detail request** is the coach's request that the client revisit named questions of her submitted onboarding, with a note on what is missing; a client has at most one open detail request at a time, and it closes when she answers it. A client's **client profile** is who she is and the coaching facts about her, and her **client settings** are her preferences (Business Rules 68–72). Her weight stays in her dated measurement entries: her starting weight is her first entry and her current weight her latest.

6. **Three coaching bundles: 1 Month, 3 Months, and 6 Months.** Longer commitments have lower per-month pricing; all bundles include the same benefits. Pricing is public, but checkout is available only through a payment link. The coach can send a payment link only for an assessment call that has ended. A payment link works for 30 days from when it is sent; a re-send replaces it and the earlier link stops working at once; a payment spends it. The platform records at most one payment per assessment call. Before paying, the visitor chooses a coaching bundle and a start choice; the start choice has no default and is required. The payment is a recurring subscription charged in euros once per bundle length (every 1, 3, or 6 months) at her price tier, and the buyer's email is fixed to the call's email. A completed payment creates the client, her client profile with the identity she booked the call with (first name, last name, email, date of birth, gender, country, and phone; Business Rule 68), and a coaching subscription that has not started. The primary goal she booked with stays on her assessment call. The payment provider's receipt is the payment record; the platform sends no receipt. A client may hold several coaching subscriptions over time, at most one that is not ended.
7. **Every coaching subscription has a 14-day withdrawal period from the payment.** Her start choice decides whether her program starts immediately or once the withdrawal period ends (Business Rule 6). While her start choice is to start after the withdrawal period and that period has not ended, she can ask the coach to start now: her start choice changes to starting immediately, and from then on she cancels under the terms of that start choice. The withdrawal period applies to her first purchase only. Cancellation, refunds, renewals, and the end of her coaching subscription follow Business Rules 63–67.
8. **The coach sees each client's coaching subscription**: its coaching bundle, payment date, start choice, whether her program has started, whether she paid the reduced price, when it ends or ended once it is cancelled or has ended, and any refund due until it is refunded. She also sees each ended assessment call's sales state.

## Assessment Calls

An **Assessment Call** is a free 30-minute video call between a visitor and the coach, booked from the public site without an account. It has the visitor's first name, last name, email, date of birth, gender, primary goal, country, optional phone, and optional notes, a start time, the visitor's time zone, the coach's time zone, and a join link. The **primary goal** is the visitor's own stated aim, one of Lose weight, Build muscle, Build strength, or Maintain but improve lifestyle; it is distinct from the coach-created Goal. The public site words the call simply as "call"; "Assessment Call" is the product's name for it.

9. **Every assessment call lasts 30 minutes and reserves the coach for a full hour.** Slots start on the hour, one per hour, because each call reserves the 30 minutes after it as a buffer. The visitor sees only the 30-minute call; the buffer and the 60-minute reservation are never shown to her.
10. **The coach sets her availability: the weekdays and the daily hour interval in which she takes assessment calls and check-ins.** Any of the seven weekdays can be chosen, and at least one must be. Start and end are whole hours between 00:00 and 24:00 with the start before the end; slots start on the hour from the start hour, the last one an hour before the end hour (17:00 to 20:00 yields 17:00, 18:00, and 19:00). The hours are the coach's own local time and are saved together with her time zone. Until she saves for the first time, availability is Monday to Friday, 17:00 to 20:00 Europe/Bucharest. One availability serves both: a check-in is offered inside the same weekdays and hours (Business Rule 45). A change applies only to what is offered from then on: calls already booked stay booked, keep blocking their hour, and stay listed, and so do check-ins already requested or approved.
11. **A slot is offered only when it lies inside the coach's availability, starts at least two hours from now, falls within the next 30 days, and is not taken.** A slot is taken when its hour overlaps time already reserved by a booked call or by a pending or approved check-in (Business Rule 45). A taken slot is refused identically whoever holds it, including the visitor's own earlier booking, so the answer never reveals who holds a slot. A day without an open slot cannot be selected.
12. **One email address holds at most one upcoming assessment call.** A booking for an address that already holds one is refused with a generic message that reveals nothing about the existing call: no date, time, join link, or booking reference. Past calls do not block a new booking.
13. **A booking sends two emails: a confirmation to the visitor and a notification to the coach.** The visitor's confirmation greets her by first name. The coach's notification carries the visitor's full name, email, phone when given, age with date of birth, gender, primary goal, and country. Each carries her notes when given, the call's date and time in the recipient's time zone with the zone named, the 30-minute duration, the join link, an add-to-calendar action for Google Calendar, and a calendar file that opens in Apple Calendar, Outlook, and Google Calendar in the recipient's local time. Nothing is written to the coach's calendar and no reminder is sent.
14. **Every assessment call meets in the coach's single meeting room.** The coach sets the meeting room's link in her settings: one absolute `https` URL, or empty while she has none. Each call has a join link, a platform link for that call that stays valid indefinitely and redirects, when opened, to the meeting room current at that moment, so a room change reaches every call, including those in emails already sent. Approved check-ins meet in the same room (Business Rule 49). While no meeting room is set, the join link of a known call shows a page saying the call link is not ready yet and reveals no call detail; the join link of an unknown call shows a privacy-safe not-found page. A join link keeps working in waiting list mode.
15. **Every time shown to a person is in her own local time zone, and stored times keep their zone.** On screen, the booking page, its confirmation, and the coach portal render times in the viewer's browser zone without naming it. Both emails render the time in the recipient's zone and name it. Every call records the visitor's time zone and the coach's time zone at booking, and every stored moment is an instant.

## Waiting List

16. **Waiting list mode is an operator-controlled feature flag.** While enabled, coaching CTAs are hidden and the free store and all landing page content stay available. The persisted pre-launch default is enabled; an absent flag means normal coaching mode. If the flag cannot be read, the public site uses waiting list mode without making an availability claim.
17. **Every accepted submission joins one waitlist; allocation determines pricing.** A limited number of reduced-price places exist. The allocation recorded at submission decides whether the visitor receives reduced pricing on every coaching bundle or regular pricing. Joining stays open after reduced-price places run out. The number of reduced-price places cannot be lowered while more people hold reduced-price places than the new number allows.
18. **Public availability is qualitative, delayed, and privacy-preserving.** Public surfaces show exactly one of "Reduced-price spots available", "Limited spots", or "Reduced-price spots closed", refreshed at fixed half-hour intervals. They never expose a count, progress toward capacity, or an immediate change after a submission. Reduced prices and promotional copy appear only while availability is "available" or "limited". If availability cannot be determined, a generic outage message is shown, signup stays open, and no reduced-price claim is made.
19. **Duplicate submissions look identical to new ones.** Re-submitting a registered email keeps its existing allocation, refreshes consent and retention, and sends no additional confirmation email. The visitor is shown the same generic confirmation for new and duplicate submissions.
20. **Validation, bot verification, and server failures are real error outcomes.** Invalid emails stay on the form for correction, bot-failed submissions are rejected, and server failures ask the visitor to retry and offer the support email. A newly accepted regular-pricing entrant receives a confirmation email stating that joining succeeded, reduced-price places were already full, and the signup does not include reduced pricing.

## Digital Store

21. **The store is public.** Products are free or paid, and each shows which it is. A paid product has a fixed price in euros and a fixed price in US dollars; the store shows prices in the currency the visitor chooses, euros until she chooses, remembers her choice across pages and visits, and the shown price includes any applicable tax. A product's prices can change, and a product can become paid or free again; nothing already acquired or bought is affected. Product types include e-books, workout challenges, nutrition tips and recipes, workout plans, nutrition plans, and fat loss plans. Products can be inspected before acquisition and added to a persistent cart.
22. **Acquisition requires an email, acceptance of the current Terms, and bot verification.** The Privacy Policy is a linked notice, not a choice. Marketing consent is a separate, optional, unchecked choice that never blocks delivery. **Buying a paid product additionally requires the visitor's express request that delivery begin at once and her acknowledgement that she loses the statutory withdrawal right by it.** Payment is taken by the payment provider on its own page, where the entered email is shown and cannot be changed; the platform never holds card details. The whole cart is paid in one payment in the chosen currency, free items included at no charge, and nothing is delivered before the payment provider confirms the payment.
23. **Outcomes are explicit.** An invalid email, failed bot verification, failed delivery, and server failure each produce a clear message, and failures keep the visitor's selections and details for retry. If a requested product is no longer available, the whole request is rejected, the cart drops the unavailable items, and the visitor is asked to review and retry. Successful free requests confirm the resources were sent to the email, without order or price framing. A payment the visitor abandons on the provider's page returns her to the store with her cart intact and a clear message. A successful purchase confirms the order and that the download link is in the email, and offers to send the order email again.
24. **One delivery email per accepted request** offers a single primary download action for all granted resources. Download access is reached from that email, lasts seven days from each request, and can be revoked. Invalid, expired, or revoked links show one privacy-safe unavailable message that does not reveal what the link pointed to, and the visitor can request the resources again. A completed purchase sends one order email that names each product, its amount and currency, the total, and the Terms version accepted, with the same single download action for everything in the order; it is an order confirmation, not a fiscal invoice. A refund is issued only by the coach through the payment provider and revokes the order's download access; the platform sends no refund message of its own, and the provider's refund receipt is the buyer's notice. A refunded order's confirmation page no longer shows the order.
25. **Delivery is rate-limited per email address**: at most one delivery per minute and ten in any rolling 24 hours. Addresses differing only by a sub-address tag share one allowance. A declined request explains which limit was reached, records nothing, and keeps the visitor's selections. A delivery that fails or whose outcome is unknown does not consume the allowance. Purchases are not rate-limited.
26. **Acquisitions belong to the entered email.** An acquisition or a purchase is recorded against the email the visitor enters and is never linked to an account. Signed-in visitors acquire products exactly as signed-out visitors do, by entering an email.
27. **Lost access is recovered by acquiring again.** A visitor who no longer has a delivery email requests a free product again from the store, or buys a paid product again. The platform never restores access from an account.

## Programs

28. **A client has one program**, tied to a goal (prefilled from the primary goal on her client profile, editable by the coach) and written in one language, English or Romanian, chosen by the coach when the program is created; platform chrome stays English. The program is built only for a client whose onboarding answers are approved and whose coaching has not ended.
29. **The plan is the coach's sequence of blocks.** Each block has a type (Foundation, Hypertrophy, Strength, Deload), a whole number of weeks, a split, its training days (2 to 5), a target session length, a weekly calendar naming a session, a cardio session or rest for each weekday, sessions that repeat every week of the block with the same exercises (only a row's sets, warm-up and working, may differ by week, Business Rule 34), and a daily steps target. The split is a template, Full Body or Upper/Lower, that sets the block's sessions, the movement patterns it suggests for each slot of a session, and the weekly layout. A Full Body session opens with a squat or a hinge as its main lift, alternating from one session to the next, follows with the other lower-body pattern or a lunge, pairs a push and a pull as a superset, and ends with core; Lower A is knee-dominant, Lower B hip-dominant, Upper A push-led and Upper B pull-led. Changing a block's training days re-lays its week and its sessions, and the coach can rename its split. The plan's length is the sum of its blocks. Any block for any client: the client's level (from her client profile, Business Rule 69) only drives suggestions.
30. **Progress is calendar driven.** Day 1 of the program and of the subscription is the moment version 1 is published. Plan weeks are seven-day spans from day 1; the current block and "week X of Y" follow the calendar, never the versions. Check-in form days fall every 14 days from day 1, each on the first day on or after its date that falls on her preferred check-in day (Business Rule 72).
31. **A version is a frozen snapshot.** A program has at most one draft and exactly one published version at a time; earlier versions are superseded and kept read-only with their PDF. A published version is never edited; any change, including a correction, is a new version prefilled from the current one. From version 2, "What changed and why" is mandatory and shown to the client until she dismisses it. Publishing a new version emails the client.
32. **Publishing needs the sessions that come next.** Every session on the calendar of every block that overlaps the span from the publish day to the next check-in form day, and at least 14 days, holds at least one exercise, and every cardio day in that span holds at least one segment. Blocks further ahead may stay empty and must be filled and published before they start; the coach is told when an empty block starts within sight. A client who chose to start after the withdrawal period cannot have a version published before her start date. Publishing is also refused while a new version makes the current block shorter than the weeks it has already run.
33. **Sequence rules.** Publishing is refused while Foundation is not the first block, the plan starts with Deload, two Deloads follow each other, or a Deload is not exactly one week. The coach is warned, and may publish anyway, on more Hypertrophy and Strength weeks in a row without a Deload than her level supports (10 for a beginner, 6 for an intermediate client, 5 for an advanced one; Foundation weeks are not counted and a Foundation block does not reset the count, only a Deload does), Strength for a client whose level is beginner, a large muscle group trained on fewer than two days a week, two hard days in a row without a rest or easy-cardio day, a cardio session with hard rounds the day before a heavy leg session, more than two such cardio sessions a week, a superset of two exercises on the same primary muscle, or a session estimated longer than its block's target.
34. **Blocks set the defaults; the coach keeps the last word.** Every block type carries default sets, reps, RIR and rest per exercise role; in a Hypertrophy or Strength block every working set of an exercise starts at RIR 3 except its last working set, which starts at RIR 2 (an exercise with a single working set starts at RIR 2). A set is a warm-up set (reps and an optional suggested weight) or a working set (a reps range, one RIR, a whole number, and an optional suggested weight; without a weight, the client picks one that matches the RIR); a timed exercise counts seconds instead of reps. Rest is per exercise. An exercise's sets, reps, RIR and rest are its **targets**. The coach sets every week of the block in one place. Each week of a block has its own sets, warm-up and working, in the order performed: a warm-up set has its reps and suggested weight, a working set its reps, RIR and suggested weight. The coach sets each set of a week individually, adds and removes sets in a week, and can turn a set from a warm-up set into a working set or back; a week may hold different working sets (for example a heavier first set followed by lighter ones). A row's rest and everything else about it stay the same every week. The block's first week is where a row's plan starts: it starts from the block's defaults and the coach sets it. Every later week is suggested from the first week: the same sets, with working-set targets progressed under the row's progression rule and the client's level, and suggested warm-up weights following that week's working sets. What the coach changes in a later week stays in that week; it changes no other week, and the values she has not touched in that week keep following the first week. For a row on double progression (Business Rule 35) the builder suggests each later week's working-set targets from the first week, the client's level and the block type: reps rise through the reps range and the suggested weight rises once the range allows it, faster for a beginner than for an intermediate client and slowest for an advanced one; a suggested weight is always one the exercise's equipment can be loaded to (an empty barbell is 20 kg and its smallest plate 2.5 kg a side, so barbell weights move in 5 kg steps from 20 kg; dumbbells move in 1 kg steps, kettlebells in 4 kg, and machines, cables and everything else in 2.5 kg; these are product defaults, and the coach cannot configure the equipment) and never falls from one week to the next. In a Hypertrophy or Strength block of three weeks or more, the RIR of each working set falls week by week, never below 1 in Hypertrophy or 2 in Strength (a set already at the floor stays there); it stays the same every week for a beginner and in a Foundation block. A working set without a suggested weight progresses in reps and RIR only, a timed exercise progresses in seconds, and an exercise counted in distance or breaths does not progress. When a week cannot progress as suggested (the next weight is too large a step, or the top of the range is reached without weight to add), it holds its targets and says why. A row on a custom progression rule gets no suggestions: every week keeps the first week's sets unless the coach types others for that week. A value the coach types stays as typed. A Deload block prefills from the last week of the block before it: the same exercises with that week's warm-up sets and the number of its working sets halved, RIR 4 and suggested weights reduced by a tenth. A default or a weekly suggestion fills only values the coach has not typed, and the coach can override every value. Every client-facing text (block intro, calibration, split, warm-up, cool-down, legend, progression, steps, cycle, missed-day note) is a template in both languages, copied into the program and editable per client; until the Romanian templates are written, a Romanian program starts from the English text, marked to the coach as pending. A block other than Deload may mark its first week as a calibration week, which changes the texts and the tracking table's first column; the coach sets its sets, reps and RIR like any other week, it shows no suggested weight and the client finds her weights by RIR; suggested weights are based on the block's second week.
35. **A session is slot groups of exercise rows** from the exercise library: Main lift, Secondary, Accessories (in supersets) and Core or finisher; a slot names the movement patterns of the exercises it holds, and the coach chooses any exercise for any slot. The coach places an exercise from the library in a slot group, reorders the rows and groups two into a superset. A row holds its sets (Business Rule 34), its rest, optionally a tempo in words and a last-set technique, up to two substitutions suggested from the library by movement pattern, primary muscle and the equipment on her client profile, a progression rule (double progression by default, from which the builder suggests each later week's targets, or a custom rule in the coach's own words, Business Rule 34) and the library's cues. Her equipment choices stand for the library equipment they cover: Full gym for all of it, Machines for machine, cable and Smith machine, Barbell for barbell, squat rack, bench, EZ bar and trap bar, Dumbbells for dumbbells and bench, Kettlebells for kettlebell, Resistance bands for bands, and Bodyweight only for none. A session's warm-up is a list of exercises from the library, each with sets and reps or seconds; its cool-down, when switched on, is a list of stretches from the library, each held for a number of seconds, with no free text. A session shows its estimated length against its block's target. Every change that discards content (the split, the training days, a day's kind, resetting a block to its split, removing a block or a session) asks first and names what is lost.
36. **Cardio and the cycle.** A cardio day holds a cardio session: a named, ordered list of segments on exercises from the library. A segment is steady (one exercise, minutes and an intensity) or rounds (an ordered list of one or more exercises, each with work seconds or reps, an easy time after each exercise, a rest between rounds, the number of rounds set once, and an intensity). Easy cardio, moderate cardio, intervals and finisher are presets that build segments the coach then edits. Conditioning exercises (tagged Conditioning in the library) appear in both cardio and resistance sessions. The daily steps target sits on the block (Business Rule 29). The program's cycle mode, adapted to symptoms or general, starts from the cycle mode on her client profile (Business Rule 69) and the coach may change it for the program; in adapted mode the coach sets adjustment rules for the client's symptom periods (keep, RIR +1, one set less, HIIT to LISS, low-energy session) and the client sees her estimated cycle day from her onboarding answers. The low-energy session is part of every program.
37. **The client sees her program from publication.** She is shown what changed in a version until she dismisses it. She sees the program's focus, the coach's personal note, the plan's blocks and weeks, the week of the plan she is in, the next check-in form day and, in adapted mode, her estimated cycle day. She can read any plan week, the current one first; a week is the seven days of a plan week counted from day 1 (Business Rule 30) and belongs to one block. A session gives its warm-up, its exercises with every set, at the targets of the week she is reading, and their rest, and its cool-down; a cardio day gives its segments, whose intensity is read on the same effort scale as RIR. Her daily steps target applies to every day of the week. Working sets are numbered from 1 and warm-up sets are marked as warm-up apart from them, the same in the builder, on her Program page and in the PDF. Every exercise, in a session, a warm-up, a cool-down or a cardio segment, gives her that exercise's detail: its video, its targets, its cues, the muscles it works, its equipment, its substitutions, each with its own detail, and how to progress. She receives every client-facing text of the version; the calibration text applies to the sessions of a calibration week. She sees her published versions, each with its PDF, once there is more than one; she never sees a draft. A PDF is generated once at publication. It holds the whole version, every text included, and gives her a place to record each set by hand, which she photographs for the check-in form. It is stored with the version and downloadable only from her account and the coach's.
38. **Nutrition travels with the version.** A version carries a nutrition section at the same cadence; it is in the MVP, but its structure, a meal plan built from the food and recipe libraries, is not yet specified (Open Question 9). Until it is, the section holds the coach's nutrition notes. When screening declared a chronic condition or chronic medication, the section carries no individualised recommendations and the coach decides.
39. **Post-MVP on this model:** logging sets per row and week in the app, program templates (program content without a client), in-app substitutions during a workout, workout history and review, and system messages for program events.
40. **Exercise videos are raw `.mp4` or `.mov` uploads of up to 50 MB.** A video is private: only the coach plays it today, from any point in it, and the clients will see an exercise's video only through the plans that use it. A replaced or removed video is deleted. This rule is in the MVP with the exercise library.
41. **System messages record plan and scheduling events Post-MVP.** Creating or updating a client's plan, and every check-in event (request, approval, reschedule, cancellation, coach-initiated check-in), sends a message in the coach–client chat thread and notifies the relevant party. In the MVP, check-in events still notify the relevant party and remain visible in the check-in flow without creating a chat entry.

## Check-ins

A **Check-in** has a client, a coach, a date and time, a kind (`recurring`, `ad-hoc`, or, Post-MVP, `program review`), a status (`pending`, `approved`, `clash`, `passed`, or `cancelled`), who initiated it, who proposed its current time, an optional linked plan, an optional note of up to 500 characters from either party, and a reschedule count. A check-in lasts one hour and starts on the hour. A check-in is pending until the other party agrees to its time, approved once agreed, and passed once its approved time is over; whether the meeting actually took place is not tracked. A check-in that was moved keeps its earlier time and is shown as rescheduled. A weekly check-in whose hour cannot be held is a clash until the coach gives it a new time (Business Rule 42).

42. **Recurring check-ins are auto-approved.** In the MVP, the coach sets a weekly check-in series for a client from the client's record, only for a client who can answer (Business Rule 43). A client has at most one series. Its weeks run from the first matching weekday that starts at least 24 hours away and not before her program start, to the end of her paid period, and never at or after her coaching end. The series' weekday is suggested from her preferred check-in day (Business Rule 72). Each week is approved without an answer and holds its hour (Business Rule 45). A week whose hour is already taken, or that falls outside the coach's availability, is a clash: it holds no hour, it counts as awaiting the coach's answer, and the coach reschedules it. Her new time is proposed to the client as any new time (Business Rule 46) and does not use one of the two new times of Business Rule 48. A clash nobody reschedules is cancelled when its time arrives, without an email; both parties' notification bells say it was missed. A renewal of her coaching adds the weeks of the new paid period, and starting her program early adds the weeks that were missing. Changing the series replaces every future week from the next matching day, those already moved or waiting for an answer included; weeks already past stay. Stopping the series cancels every future week and frees their hours. The client is emailed once when the series is set up, changed, and stopped (Notifications item 3). Only the coach sees the series as a whole. Both the client's and the coach's Upcoming list every week to come; the client moves only her next weekly check-in and cancels none, while the coach moves or cancels any week from her Check-ins page. Other frequencies are deferred. The program review call within 72 hours of version 1 and the two-week check-in form are specified in a later round (Open Question 5).
43. **Ad-hoc check-ins require approval from the other party.** The client asks for one from her Check-ins page; the coach schedules one from the client's record, only for a client who can answer: one who has sent her onboarding and whose coaching has not ended. Post-MVP, they can also initiate them from the messaging area or chat. Either party picks a time offered under Business Rule 45 and may add a note, which the other party sees attributed to its author. The party who did not request the check-in approves or declines it, and the party who requested it may withdraw it while it is pending. A client whose coaching has ended cannot ask for a check-in (Business Rule 67).
44. **A client has at most one pending ad-hoc request at a time.** Only an ad-hoc check-in she requested that is still pending counts; a check-in the coach requested and a check-in either party has moved to a new time do not, and the coach's requests have no such limit. While one is pending, the client cannot submit another and is told why. A pending request holds its hour (Business Rule 45).
45. **Scheduling uses coach availability.** Check-in times are offered inside the coach's saved availability (Business Rule 10): the same weekdays and hours, and the same one schedule, as assessment calls. They start on the hour from the start hour, the last one an hour before the end hour. A time is offered only when it starts at least 24 hours from now and falls within the next 30 days, and a day without an offered time cannot be selected. The weeks of a weekly check-in series are not limited to the 30 days; the 24 hours apply to its first week. A pending request holds its hour: no assessment call can be booked over it and no other request can take it. An approved check-in keeps holding its hour. A declined, withdrawn, or cancelled check-in frees it. Likewise, a booked assessment call takes its hour from the times offered for check-ins. These rules apply to new check-ins and reschedules alike.
46. **Either party can propose a new time for an approved check-in, or for a pending check-in that awaits their answer.** Of a weekly check-in series the client may move only her next weekly check-in; the coach may move any week (Business Rule 42). The new time is picked from the times offered under Business Rule 45; the earlier hour is freed and the new one held at once, and a new time whose hour was taken meanwhile is refused and leaves the check-in as it was. The check-in is pending again and awaits the other party, who can accept the new time, decline it, or propose another one. Accepting approves the check-in at the new time. A proposal may carry an optional note. The proposer cannot take a new time back; a new time left unanswered when its start arrives is cancelled without an email, and both parties' notification bells say it expired. Post-MVP, that note also appears in the chat thread.
47. **Cancellation is final.** Declining a request or a new time cancels the check-in; it never reverts to the original time. The party who sent a new request can withdraw it while it is pending. Who may cancel an approved check-in depends on its kind: only the party who requested an ad-hoc check-in may cancel it once approved, after confirming; the other party may propose a new time (Business Rule 46). The coach may cancel any other approved check-in except a program review, and so can cancel any week of a weekly check-in series, a clashing one included. The client cannot cancel a weekly check-in. A cancelled check-in frees its hour. A pending check-in that reaches its time unanswered is cancelled automatically, without an email; both parties' notification bells say it expired. As soon as a client's coaching has an end date, whether she cancels or the payment provider sets or reaches the end, every pending, approved, or clashing check-in starting at or after that end is cancelled and its hour freed, without any message; if her coaching is later kept after all, those check-ins stay cancelled. No check-in time at or after her coaching end is offered to her requests, to the coach's scheduling, or to a new time.
48. **A check-in gets at most two new times over its whole life.** The original request does not count. After two new times, a pending check-in can only be accepted or declined, and an approved one can only be kept or, where Business Rule 47 allows it, cancelled.
49. **Check-ins meet on Google Meet, in the coach's meeting room.** The client and coach portals each offer a "Join Meet" link for every approved check-in until it ends; it becomes the main action from 10 minutes before the start until the check-in ends. The join link is a platform link that opens the coach's meeting room current at that moment (Business Rule 14), and only the check-in's client and the coach can use it. While no meeting room is set, the join link says it is not ready yet. A check-in that is not approved, or whose time is over, has no join link.

## Menstrual Cycle

50. **Every female client has a menstrual cycle profile**, created during self-onboarding and visible to the coach. It records whether she currently gets a period and how regular it is, her contraception, whether she is pregnant, postpartum, or breastfeeding, and whether she is in perimenopause or menopause. When she gets a period, it records her average cycle length, or her shortest and longest cycle when it is irregular, and the day her last period started; she may say she is not sure of her cycle length or her last period start. It also records any gynecological condition a doctor has diagnosed, the symptoms that come back regularly, and whether she tracks her cycle in an app. Approval sets the cycle mode on her client profile from these answers (Business Rule 69), and the coach may change it; her program starts from it (Business Rule 36).
51. Post-MVP: **Current cycle phase is derived** from the last logged period and the client's average cycle length, and shown on the client dashboard and the coach's client detail page.
52. **Clients without an active cycle are fully supported.** Cycle tracking and cycle-driven adjustments are gracefully skipped or replaced with non-cycle-based coaching.

## Legal and Public Submissions

53. **The current Privacy Policy and Terms & Conditions are dedicated public pages** at `/privacy` and `/terms`, each showing its version and effective date. Every public page links to both, in normal and waiting list mode.
54. **Every public submission rejects bot-driven attempts before it affects system state.** This covers waitlist capture (hero, footer, pricing page), store acquisition for logged-out buyers, assessment call booking, and any future public submission point. The mechanism must offer accessible alternatives or require no visual or motor input, in keeping with the WCAG AA target.

## Client Resources

56. **A resource belongs to one client.** The coach gives a client a file with a title, an optional description, and tags. Only the coach and that client can see or download it.
57. **A resource is one file:** a PDF of at most 50 pages, an image, or a Word or Excel document, up to 25 MB. A file of another type or over a limit is refused with a clear message, and the coach keeps what she entered.
58. **PDFs and images can be read in the portal, page by page. Word and Excel documents are download only.** Every resource can be downloaded as the original file.
59. **Tags are one vocabulary across all of the coach's clients.** Tags that differ only in letter case are the same tag, a resource holds a tag once, and a tag exists only while a resource carries it.
60. **The coach can change a resource's title, description, and tags, or delete it.** The file itself is not replaced. Deleting asks for confirmation and removes the resource for the client at once.
61. **A resource is new for the client until she opens it.**
62. **A client's resources follow her portal access.** Once her coaching ends she can no longer reach them (Business Rule 3); the coach keeps them on the client's record.

## Coaching Subscriptions

A coaching subscription is **cancelled** when it will no longer renew and the client keeps her access until a set date; it has **ended** once her access is over, on that date or at once when a cancellation ends it immediately. A **refund due** is what the coach owes a client after her cancellation: an amount in euros, the reason it is owed, and a deadline 14 days after the cancellation. A **payment problem** is a renewal payment that failed and has not been paid since. The payment provider holds payments, renewals, refunds, and the client's card; the platform never holds card details.

63. **A client can cancel her coaching subscription herself, on terms set by her start choice and when she cancels.** When her start choice is to start after the withdrawal period and that period has not ended, her coaching subscription ends at once and she is refunded in full. In every other case she is not refunded, whether her start choice is to start immediately or the withdrawal period has ended: her coaching subscription is cancelled, and she keeps access until her payment date plus her coaching bundle's length, or, once her program has started, until the end of the bundle length she has already paid for, when it ends. A coaching subscription that is already cancelled or ended has nothing to cancel.
64. **Refunds are issued by the coach in the payment provider; the platform tells the coach what is due and records what was refunded.** A cancellation with a refund records a refund due, emails the coach its amount and deadline, and marks the client as needing a refund. The coach sees what is still due until the payment provider reports it refunded: a partial refund lowers what is due, and a full refund records the date she was refunded and clears the marker. A refund the coach issues when none is due is recorded as refunded too. The coach has no cancel or refund action in the platform.
65. **No renewal is charged before her program starts.** On her first purchase, from her payment until her program starts, renewal is held and nobody is charged; the hold ends when her program starts. The withdrawal period and the hold apply to her first purchase only, never to a renewal. A failed renewal payment shows the client a payment problem until a renewal is paid; the payment provider retries the payment and emails her. Until her coaching subscription ends she can update her card on the payment provider's hosted page, reached from the client portal. She sees which card is on file (brand, last four digits and expiry).
66. **Cancellations and endings decided in the payment provider are mirrored by the platform.** When the coach cancels a coaching subscription in the payment provider, or the payment provider cancels it after every retry of a failed renewal has failed, the platform applies it as soon as the provider reports it: a cancellation set for a later date makes the coaching subscription cancelled until that date, lifting that cancellation restores it, and an immediate cancellation ends it. Such an ending records no refund due.
67. **When her coaching subscription has ended, her portal access ends; her account and her history stay.** Every portal page leads her to one page saying her coaching has ended, with no action. While a refund is due, that page also tells her the coach will refund her and when to expect it.

## Client Profile and Client Settings

A client's **client profile** holds who she is (first name, last name, email, phone, date of birth, gender, country, and her picture), her **coaching facts** (height, training days per week, minutes per session, available equipment, training place, limitations, level, primary goal, goal weight, lifestyle activity level, cycle mode, and dietary restrictions), and the coach's **coach notes**. Her **client settings** hold her units, her **preferred check-in day**, and her **preferred channel** (Email or WhatsApp). Her onboarding submission stays the frozen record of her answers; every flow that needs a fact about her or a preference reads her client profile or client settings, never the answers, except her program's estimated cycle day (Business Rule 36).

68. **Her client profile and client settings exist from the moment her payment creates the client.** Her identity is the one she booked her assessment call with (Business Rule 6) and her picture is empty; her units are metric until she chooses hers in onboarding (Accounts and Onboarding item 9). Her coaching facts, coach notes, and preferences stay empty until her answers are approved. Sending her onboarding and answering a detail request leave her client profile unchanged.
69. **Approval fills her coaching facts and her preferences from the approved answers, once.** Nothing is taken from her answers again afterwards. Each fact comes from its matching answer, with these rules: her level is intermediate when she answered that she trains consistently and follows a structured program, and beginner otherwise; advanced is never suggested. Her cycle mode is general for a male client, a client without cycle answers, or one who gets no period, is on the combined pill, or is in perimenopause or menopause, and adapted otherwise. Her limitations are the bone or joint problem she named when she answered Yes to having one, and empty otherwise. Her dietary restrictions combine her eating style and her allergies or intolerances, and read None when neither applies. Her preferred check-in day and preferred channel are the ones she answered. Her available equipment is one or more of Full gym, Machines, Barbell, Dumbbells, Kettlebells, Resistance bands, and Bodyweight only; Bodyweight only cannot be combined with another choice, in onboarding and on her client profile alike. Until approval, the coach's views say her coaching facts and preferences fill in once her answers are approved, and the client's views leave them out.
70. **She and the coach both edit her shared fields; the coach alone edits the rest.** The shared fields are her first and last name, phone, date of birth, country, picture, height, training days per week (2, 3, 4, or 5 or more), minutes per session (30, 45, or 60), available equipment, training place (Home, Gym, or Both), and limitations. Only the coach edits her gender, level (beginner, intermediate, or advanced), primary goal, goal weight, lifestyle activity level, cycle mode (adapted or general), dietary restrictions, and coach notes; the client sees these read-only, except the coach notes, which are private to the coach and never shown to her. Identity fields can be edited from the moment the client exists, coaching facts and coach notes from approval. Her email is read-only for everyone: it is how she signs in and how the payment provider knows her. A gender change does not reopen the cycle part of her onboarding. Her age follows her date of birth; the onboarding's age rule for the safety questions applies only while she onboards. Height is from 120 to 230 cm, goal weight from 30 to 300 kg, and limitations are at most 500 characters; a refused save explains the problem at its field and keeps what was entered. Height and goal weight are kept in metric and shown in the units of whoever views them.
71. **Her picture is uploaded or removed by her or by the coach.** It is a JPEG, PNG, or WebP image of up to 10 MB, the same images accepted as progress photos; another file is refused with a clear message. Removing it asks for confirmation. Without a picture, her initials stand in.
72. **Her preferences are read-only in the MVP.** Nobody changes her preferred check-in day or preferred channel in the MVP; she sees them in her client settings with a note that changes go through her coach for now, and the coach sees them on her client profile page. She changes her units at any time (Client Portal item 16).

Cancelling asks her to confirm and lets her keep her coaching instead. A payment problem is shown here too.

### Resources (`/client/resources`)

18. The portal tells her while she has resources she has not opened. Her resources show newest first, each with its file type, title, and tags, and marked new until she opens it (Business Rule 61).
19. She narrows them by tag and by a search on the title; the tags offered are those on her own resources. The page distinguishes no resources yet from no matches.
20. Opening a resource shows its pages when it can be read in the portal (Business Rule 58), with its description, tags, file type, size, and the date it was added, and a download action.

### Profile (`/client/profile`)

21. Her profile page shows her picture, which she uploads or removes (Business Rule 71), and her first and last name, phone, date of birth, and country, which she edits, with her email read-only. Once her answers are approved, she also edits her height, training days per week, minutes per session, available equipment, training place, and limitations, and sees her gender, level, primary goal, goal weight, lifestyle activity level, cycle mode, and dietary restrictions read-only as kept up to date by her coach; the coach notes never appear (Business Rule 70). Her measurements are on this page too. A save confirms; a refused or failed save keeps her entries.

## 5. Coach Portal (`/coach`)

### Dashboard

1. States how many assessment calls the coach still has today (calls dated today that have not ended) and lists her next three calls that have not ended, soonest first, each with the visitor's name, the call's date and time, whether it is today, and its join link, with the way to the full assessment calls list. With no upcoming call it says so and still leads to the list.
2. Managed clients, pending check-ins, and important client information. It lists up to three check-ins awaiting the coach's answer, soonest first, states how many there are, and opens each of them on the coach's Check-ins page.

### Assessment calls (`/coach/assessment-calls`)

3. Lists every assessment call with the visitor's full name, her email, her phone when given, her age with date of birth, gender, primary goal, and country, the call's date and time, her notes when she left any, whether the call is today, and its join link while the call has not ended. A call is upcoming until it ends and past afterwards; upcoming and past calls are told apart, upcoming calls list soonest first, past calls list most recent first, and past calls carry no join link. A filter offers All (the default), Upcoming, Today, and Past. A Status filter narrows the list to one sales state (held, payment link sent, or paid) or shows all of them (the default), and each option shows how many calls it matches under the current filter and search. The coach sorts the list by scheduled date, booking date, name, or email, in either direction: dates start soonest or newest first and text starts A to Z, and reversing the scheduled date reverses the whole listing order. A search narrows the list by the visitor's first name, last name, or email. The list shows ten calls per page and which of how many are shown; the active filters, sort, search, and page survive a reload and a return from another page, and the page resets to the first when a filter, the sort, or the search changes. The list says why it is empty: no upcoming calls, no calls today, no past calls, no calls yet, no matches for a search, and no calls in the chosen sales state. A call booked on `/book` appears on the next load, and a call that has ended moves from Upcoming to Past without any action. Each ended call shows its sales state (Business Rule 8). A held call offers to send a payment link and a call whose payment link was sent offers to re-send it; either asks the coach to confirm, then emails the payment link to the call's email (Business Rule 6), and an email that cannot be sent is reported so she can send it again. A paid call links, in place of a sales action, to the client detail page of the client its payment created.

### Clients (`/coach/clients`)

4. Lists every client from the moment she pays, with her picture, name and email, client status, coaching bundle, and join date (her payment date). A Status filter narrows the list to one of three groups, Onboarding (Invited through Approved), Active, and Inactive (Cancelled and Inactive), or shows all of them (the default), and each option shows how many clients it matches under the current search. A search narrows the list by name or email. The coach sorts the list by name, client status, coaching bundle, or join date; join date newest first is the default. The active filter, sort, and search survive a reload. The list distinguishes no clients yet from no matches. A client with a refund due is marked as needing a refund in the list and on her client detail page until it is refunded (Business Rule 64). Each client has a client detail page showing her picture and, from her client profile, her age, gender, country, phone, and email, with the way to her client profile page; her invitation's state with a re-send until her account exists (Business Rule 2); her coaching subscription (coaching bundle, payment date, start choice, program start, renewal, whether she paid the reduced price, when it ends or ended, and any refund due with its reason and deadline, then the date she was refunded); her client status and, once her onboarding is submitted, her answers with the safety and cycle signals that need the coach's attention and the review actions; her first measurements; and her assessment call as she booked it (its date and time, her name, email, date of birth, gender, country, and phone, the primary goal she booked with, and the notes she left when booking). The coach sets, changes, and stops the client's weekly check-in there (Business Rule 42), seeing the series' day and time, with a link to all her check-ins; she moves or cancels weeks on the Check-ins page. It also carries her program (Business Rule 28): the action to build it, the state of the draft, or the published version's current block, week and next check-in form day with the way into the program. Post-MVP it links to completed workout history.

### Messaging (`/coach/messages`) — Post-MVP

5. The coach's conversations each show the client's picture, online status, unread count, and last message. She can pin or unpin, mute or unmute, flag for follow-up, archive, and delete a conversation; deleting asks for confirmation. There is no call or video action. The coach can go from a conversation directly to that client's profile.
6. The coach sends messages and attaches files.
7. The active chat shows the next approved check-in for that client. The coach can initiate an ad-hoc check-in from here.
8. Pending requests and reschedule proposals appear in the conversation with client name, requested date and time, and optional note, and the coach accepts or declines them there. Acting updates the check-in immediately and sends a notification.

### Workout review and history — Post-MVP

9. Completed workouts are grouped by subscription, then program version, then block and week. The coach can filter by date range, session duration, session volume, and muscle groups trained (a session matches when at least one exercise trains a selected group; multiple groups may be selected). For the current selection the coach sees session count, total volume, average volume per session, and average duration.
10. Each workout review shows logged against prescribed weight and reps per set, rest taken against prescribed, swaps made, compliance percentage, duration, and volume. Volumes use the coach's unit setting.

### Check-ins (`/coach/checkins`)

11. The portal tells the coach how many check-ins await her answer. The page opens on Requests when a check-in awaits her answer, otherwise on Upcoming. Organized as on the client's page: Upcoming (approved check-ins sorted by date, every week of a weekly check-in series included, each offering Join Meet (Business Rule 49), a new time, and cancellation per Business Rule 47), Requests (every pending check-in across clients, those awaiting the coach's answer first, each with approve or accept, reschedule, and decline, then those waiting for clients, where she can withdraw a request she sent), and Past (passed and cancelled). Each says when it is empty. Filtering, sorting, and paging work as on the client's Check-ins page, and the coach can also search by client name.

### Settings (`/coach/settings`)

12. The coach's settings hold her availability (weekdays, start hour, end hour), which serves assessment calls and check-ins alike, and the meeting room link per Business Rules 10, 14, and 49, showing the defaults until she has saved once. Saving with no weekday, a start at or after the end, or an invalid link is refused with an explanation that keeps the entered values; a valid save confirms and is live at once for the next visitor or client; a server failure is reported and keeps the entered values. While the meeting room link is empty, she is warned that visitors and clients cannot join calls or check-ins until a link is set.
13. The coach chooses units for weight and height. The choice applies across her views, including workout-history volumes and the session-volume filter.

### Client resources (`/coach/clients/:id/resources`)

14. Reached from the client detail page. Lists that client's resources as the client sees them, with the same tag filter and title search, and a sort by date added (newest first by default) or title.
15. The coach adds a resource by choosing a file, with the title prefilled from the file name. While she types a tag, existing tags are suggested (Business Rule 59). A failed upload keeps her entries for retry.
16. From a resource she opens the same view the client sees, edits its details, or deletes it (Business Rule 60).

### Client profile (`/coach/clients/:id/profile`)

17. Reached from the client detail page. The coach edits the client's whole client profile: her picture (Business Rule 71) and identity, with the email read-only, from the moment the client exists; her coaching facts and the coach notes once her answers are approved; before then the page says they fill in once her answers are approved (Business Rules 69 and 70). Once approved, her preferred check-in day and preferred channel are shown read-only (Business Rule 72). Height and goal weight are entered and shown in the coach's units. A save confirms; a refused save explains each problem at its field, and a refused or failed save keeps the coach's entries.

## 6. Exercises and Programs (`/coach/training`, `/coach/clients/:id/program`)

### Data model

Every entity in this section is in the MVP.

**Exercise**: name, form cues, type (Resistance, Cardio, Mobility, Stretching, or Breathing), the unit it is measured in (reps, time, distance, or breaths; each type allows a sensible subset and sets the default, adjustable per exercise within that subset), whether it is performed one side at a time, equipment used, difficulty, primary muscles, secondary muscles, demo video (`.mp4` or `.mov`, up to 50 MB), and tags from Strength, Hypertrophy, Recovery and Conditioning. A Conditioning exercise can also be used in cardio sessions (Business Rule 36). Tags are multi-valued (a back squat is both Strength and Hypertrophy) and distinct from a client's Goal. An exercise belongs to the coach who created it, and her library holds only her own exercises. A name is at most 80 characters and unique among her active exercises, ignoring case and surrounding spaces; form cues are at most 1,000 characters. An exercise is active until the coach archives it. An exercise needs no equipment when it lists nothing. Primary muscles are required except for Cardio; a movement pattern (squat, lunge, hinge, horizontal push, vertical push, horizontal pull, vertical pull, core, carry, isolation, conditioning) and a role (main compound, secondary compound, isolation) are both required for Resistance.

**Program**: a client's program (Business Rule 28): the client, her goal, its language (English or Romanian), the cycle mode and its adjustment rules (Business Rule 36), the start day (day 1, set when version 1 is published), and its versions.

**Version**: a frozen snapshot of the program (Business Rule 31): number, status (draft, published or superseded), the blocks, the nutrition section, the "What changed and why" note, its stored PDF and its publish time.

**Block**: type (Foundation, Hypertrophy, Strength or Deload), whole number of weeks, training days (2 to 5), split (a Full Body or Upper/Lower template, renamable), target session length, weekly calendar, sessions, cardio sessions, daily steps target, optional calibration first week, and its client-facing texts in the program's language (Business Rules 29 and 34).

**Session**: a named training day of a block with its exercise rows in slot groups, a warm-up (a list of exercises, each with sets and reps or seconds), a cool-down (on or off, with stretches held for seconds) and an estimated length (Business Rule 35).

**Exercise row**: an exercise from the library in a slot group (Main lift, Secondary, Accessories, or Core or finisher), optionally in a superset, with its sets (each a warm-up or a working set, Business Rule 34, with sets that may differ by week of the block: their number, kind, reps, RIR and suggested weight), rest, tempo, last-set technique, substitutions, progression rule (double progression or custom) and cues (Business Rule 35).

**Cardio session**: a name and an ordered list of cardio segments, placed on a weekday of its block's calendar (Business Rule 36).

**Cardio segment**: steady (an exercise, minutes and an intensity) or rounds (an ordered list of one or more exercises, each with work seconds or reps, an easy time after each exercise, a rest between rounds, the number of rounds and an intensity) (Business Rule 36).

### Exercise Library

1. The coach creates and edits exercises and reuses them across programs. Saving is refused while the name is missing, already held by another of her active exercises, or over 80 characters, while the form cues are over 1,000 characters, or while no primary muscle is chosen for a type other than Cardio; each problem is explained at its field, and her entries stay. A successful save confirms and returns her to the library.
2. An exercise has at most one demo video. A file that is not an `.mp4` or `.mov`, or is over 50 MB, is refused with a clear message, and the coach keeps what she entered; a file renamed to `.mp4` or `.mov` that holds no such video is refused when she saves. She previews the chosen video and can replace or remove it before saving. The video uploads when she saves, with visible progress; an upload that does not go through keeps everything she entered and lets her try again. The library shows which exercises have a video.
3. The library supports search and filters for type, for Strength, Hypertrophy, Recovery, and Conditioning tags, and for no-equipment exercises. Tag filters combine as "any of"; the type filter narrows to one type; the no-equipment filter narrows when applied and places no constraint otherwise (no equipment-only view, since its complement is the whole library). Filters combine with search and with each other as "all of".
4. The library is ordered by name, A to Z, by default; the coach can sort it by name or by difficulty (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced) in either direction. Long libraries are split into pages.
5. The coach archives an exercise to take it out of the active library without losing it, and can view archived exercises and restore them. Archiving frees the exercise's name for another active exercise; restoring an exercise whose name an active exercise now holds restores it under a numbered name (for example "Hip thrust (2)") that she can edit later.
6. Deleting an exercise is permanent and asks for confirmation first. An exercise used in any version of a program, draft, published or superseded, whether as an exercise row, a substitution, a warm-up or cool-down exercise or in a cardio segment, cannot be deleted, and the coach is told in how many programs.

### Program builder

7. Version 1 starts from a setup in four parts: Program (the goal, one of the primary goal values, the level and the program language), Schedule (the split, training days, session length and daily steps), Equipment (the available equipment on her client profile) and Cycle (the cycle mode, absent for a male client). The goal, level, training days, session length, equipment and cycle mode are prefilled from her client profile, and saving the setup with changed equipment updates her client profile. The setup suggests a first plan that the coach then edits (Business Rule 28).
8. The plan overview shows the blocks with their dates, their numbered weeks and the check-in form days, the plan's length, its start and the next check-in form day, and lets the coach add blocks and reorder them.
9. For each block the coach sets its type, its weeks, whether its first week is a calibration week, with an explanation of what a calibration week is, the split and its name, the training days, the target session length and the daily steps, and she can copy the block from another client's program, reset it to its split, or remove it.
10. The block's week is its seven days in order. Each day is a session, a cardio day or rest, which the coach can change; a cardio day's session is built from a preset and its segments (Business Rule 36).
11. Each session shows its slot groups (Business Rule 35) together with the library, which the coach searches and narrows by type, movement pattern, muscle and the equipment on her client profile (Business Rule 35). The coach adds an exercise from the library to a slot group, groups two exercises into a superset and ungroups them, and reorders exercises. For each exercise she sets its sets and its details: tempo, last-set technique, substitutions, progression and cues. She sees every week's sets, warm-up and working, and changes any of them (Business Rule 34). Every exercise in the builder, in a session, a warm-up, a cool-down, a cardio segment or the library, gives that exercise's detail as on the client's Program page (Business Rule 37).
12. The coach sets the program's header and focus, the block texts, the cycle rules, the nutrition notes (Business Rule 38) and the lifestyle section.
13. The coach previews the program as the client sees it.
14. A publish checklist lists what Business Rules 31–33 refuse, which blocks publishing, and what they warn about, which the coach may publish past; while she edits, she sees how many warnings there are. The coach can also discard the draft.
15. The coach's program page reads the published version in the same view as the client (Business Rule 37), without the client's what-changed notice, and adds only the coach's actions: she opens the PDF document of a published version, continues the draft or starts the next version, sees every version including the draft, and goes from an exercise's detail to that exercise in her library. It also tells her when an empty block starts within sight (Business Rule 32).

## 7. Foods and Recipes (`/coach/nutrition`)

### Data model

The Tag, the Food and the Recipe are in the MVP; the meal plan inside a program version is specified separately (Open Question 9).

**Tag**: a fixed vocabulary shared by foods and recipes, in four families: Meal-time (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack, Pre-workout, Post-workout), Cycle phase (Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, Luteal), Nutrient / benefit (Iron-rich, Omega-3, Magnesium, Anti-inflammatory), and Dietary (Vegetarian, Lactose-free). The vocabulary is fixed for now: the coach chooses among its tags but does not add, rename or remove them.

**Food**: name, category (Protein, Carb, Fat, Legumes, Extra, or Seasoning), an icon, calories, protein, carbs, and fat per 100 g, a default portion in grams, and any number of tags. A food belongs to the coach who created it, and her library holds only her own foods. A name is at most 80 characters and unique among her active foods, ignoring case and surrounding spaces. Calories, protein, carbs, and fat are each from 0 to 9,999.99 with at most two decimals, and a value left blank counts as 0. The default portion is a whole number of grams from 1 to 99,999, proposed when the food enters a recipe; a portion left blank or set to 0 becomes 100 g. A food is active until the coach archives it.

**Recipe**: name, ingredients (each one of her foods with its grams and a cooking method: Raw, Boiled, Grilled, Baked, Pan-fried, or Steamed), prep and cook time in minutes, instructions, meal roles (from the Meal-time tags), other tags, and a photo (JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 10 MB) or an icon. Its calories and macros are calculated from its ingredients, each food's values per 100 g scaled to its grams and summed, and are never typed. A recipe belongs to the coach who created it, and its name follows the food name rule among her active recipes. A recipe is active until the coach archives it.

### Food and Recipe Libraries

1. The food library lists her active foods by name, A to Z, with each food's category, values per 100 g, and tags. The coach creates and edits foods; saving writes the whole food. Saving is refused while the name is missing, already held by another of her active foods, or over 80 characters, or while a value per 100 g or the default portion is out of its range; each problem is explained at its field, and her entries stay. A successful save confirms and returns her to the library.
2. The coach composes a recipe from her active foods, setting each ingredient's grams and cooking method; its calories and macros follow her ingredients as she composes. A recipe is saved with its whole content and confirmed the same way as a food.
3. Both libraries support search by name, filters by category or by tags, sorting, and pages for long lists.
4. The coach archives a food or a recipe to take it out of the active library without losing it, and can view archived items and restore them. An archived item is not editable. Archiving frees its name for another active item; restoring an item whose name an active item now holds restores it under a numbered name (for example "Oats (2)") that she can edit later. Deleting is permanent and asks for confirmation first. A food used in a recipe cannot be deleted, and the coach is told where it is used.
5. Post-MVP: swap groups of interchangeable foods, a manual override of a recipe's calculated macros, and tags suggested from a recipe's ingredients.

## 8. Notifications

1. Both portals have a notification bell, which tells her while anything is unread. Notifications are role-aware; each person sees only her own. The bell lists the latest 30 notifications from the last 90 days, newest first, and can mark them all read. In the MVP, check-in notifications link to the appropriate Check-ins page. Post-MVP, message notifications link to routes such as `/client/messages` and `/coach/messages?client=id`. Program notifications (a version published, the check-in form's availability and reminders) and the coach's signals for an unread form, an empty upcoming block and an unbooked review call are specified in a later round (Open Question 7).
2. Opening a notification marks it read and opens it without losing app state; a check-in notification opens that check-in on the reader's Check-ins page. While she has a portal page open, she is told of each new notification within about a minute and can open it from there.
3. Types: new message; check-in requested (by client or coach); check-in approved; reschedule proposed; check-in cancelled (by decline, withdrawal, or cancellation). The requesting party is notified on approval and on decline; the other party is notified when a request is withdrawn and on cancellation of an approved check-in. The bell carries every check-in event that is emailed, plus the client's weekly check-in set up, changed, or stopped, the coach's clashing weeks added by a renewal or an early program start, and, in both parties' bells, requests, new times, and clashes that reached their time unanswered; email carries only the events listed below. A request that reaches its time unanswered is cancelled with no email; both bells say it expired. In the MVP, each request, approval, decline, and withdrawal is emailed to the party that did not act, with the check-in's date and time in the recipient's time zone; an approval email carries the check-in's join link and a calendar invite. A proposed new time is emailed to the other party with the earlier and the new time in their time zone and the proposer's note; the proposer is emailed when the new time is accepted, with the join link and a calendar invite, or declined; cancelling an approved check-in is emailed to the other party. The client is emailed when the coach sets up, changes, or stops her weekly check-in, naming the day and time of the first week in her time zone and linking to her Check-ins page; the individual weeks send no email of their own, and moving one sends the usual new-time emails. Cancellations caused by the end of a client's coaching send no email and reach neither bell. An email that cannot be sent never undoes the action that triggered it.

## 9. Shared Requirements

1. The design system in code is the source of truth for visual decisions.
2. WCAG AA. Complex interactions, especially plan building, have accessible alternatives. All animations respect reduced motion.
3. Works on mobile, tablet, and desktop.
