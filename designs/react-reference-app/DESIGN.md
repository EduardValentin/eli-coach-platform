# Design System

## Theme

Evoa has a warm, premium, and modern visual identity. Soft off-white surfaces, restrained borders and shadows, and generous whitespace keep the product calm and approachable. Magenta is the primary brand and action color; teal provides a grounded counterpoint for secondary actions and recovery-oriented content. Elegant serif headings add personality, while a clean sans-serif keeps the interface practical and easy to scan.

The design balances human coaching with professional competence. Photography and small product-UI compositions may add warmth and context, but the interface itself remains clean, focused, and consistent across the public, client, and coach surfaces.

## Audience

The product is designed for women seeking personalized strength and nutrition coaching, including women with or without an active menstrual cycle. Its warm palette, editorial headings, supportive copy hierarchy, and body-aware domain colors make the experience feel personal without relying on stereotypically feminine decoration. Clear controls, calm surfaces, and predictable structure support clients during workouts as well as the coach managing detailed plans.

## Design System

The production source of truth is `packages/ui/src/styles.css` for tokens and, for components, `packages/ui`'s eleven concern subpaths (`appointments`, `calendar`, `filters`, `layout`, `lib`, `motion`, `overlays`, `portal`, `primitives`, `tabs`, `toast`), imported one at a time — the package has no root barrel. The reference app mirrors the same visual roles and may keep prototype-only tokens and composed components where the production surface does not exist yet.

A dashed outline means "you can drop here": it marks only a real drop target, through the `drop` button variant or a drop zone such as `FileDropzone`, and never a plain action. Every button shares one base: the 8px `field` corner, a 2-unit gap, no wrapping, 50% opacity when disabled, and the one focus rule below. A `Button` marked `aria-disabled="true"` keeps that disabled look while staying focusable: 50% opacity, no hover change and the not-allowed cursor. A size sets the height, a horizontal padding that narrows beside a leading or trailing icon (`px-3` for `xs`, `px-4` for `sm` and `lg-tight`, `px-5` for `md-grow`, `px-6` for `md`, `px-8` for `lg`) and a text size; `lg-tight` is for a full button that shares its row with another action, and `md-grow`, which lets its label wrap and keeps 8px above and below it, for a sheet's full-width step action such as the check-in scheduler's and for the public site's calls to action, whose labels wrap at enlarged text; the waitlist submit grows the same way from the `lg` height. A `full` button fills its row; `full-below-sm` fills it only below the `sm` breakpoint, where stacked actions take the whole width. `card` rests the button on the `card` shadow, and `raised` sets the `action` shadow lifting to `action-hover` on hover. `glass` is the one translucent tone for an action over a photo, always on a dark area: the hero, and the recipe builder's photo, whose Replace and Remove sit on a bottom scrim fading from `surface-inverted` to transparent; the header's portal link uses it while the header is transparent and `primary` once it turns solid. In the portals a button's size follows where it lives, `xs` (32px) in a table or list row, `sm` (40px) inside a widget, card or dialog footer, `md` (48px) for page header actions and form submits, and its variant follows its importance: at most one `primary` per group and always last, `outline` for alternatives, `ghost` for dismiss and icon-only controls, `destructive-outline` for a loss action in a row and solid `destructive` only inside a confirm dialog. Keyboard focus is drawn by one unlayered `:focus-visible` rule in `theme.css` rather than per component: the `focus-visible:ring-*` and `focus-visible:outline-*` utilities the primitives carry paint nothing in this app, and the primitives also carry `outline-none`, which as a utility beats anything in `@layer base`. The indicator is a 2px `--focus-ring` outline at 2px offset with a soft 16% halo, matching the brand ring production already draws. `--focus-ring` resolves to `--brand`, which clears 5.5:1 on white and 4.9:1 on the warm page background, well over the 3:1 SC 1.4.11 asks of a non-text indicator; controls inside an element marked `data-surface="inverted"` (the hero, and the header while it is transparent over it) switch to the lighter `--brand-on-inverted`, because the brand pink only reaches 2.7:1 against near-black. Menu and option items, and `tabindex="-1"` skip-link targets, are excluded from the general selector, because a menu or listbox moves focus with the arrow keys and only the items that opt in draw the ring: an option that is its own tab stop with no other focus look, such as a meal icon choice, opts back in with `data-focus-ring`, and so does every `DropdownMenu` item and `Select` option, which sets `--focus-ring-offset` to -2px and drops the halo so the ring sits inside the item's box over its fill (the checked `Select` option's ring is `--primary-foreground`, at -4px); a base rule also removes the browser outline those targets would get on programmatic focus. A sibling rule matches `label[data-chip-control]:has(:focus-visible)` with the same outline and halo: a chip whose real control is a visually hidden native input (`CheckboxChip`, and the file inputs behind `FileDropzone` and `FilePickerButton`) cannot show its own outline, since an `opacity: 0` element's outline is invisible too, so the attribute hands the same indicator to the visible label instead. `data-chip-control` is the opt-in for that case; any future chip built the same way must carry it. `TagInput` draws the indicator the same way on its field: the bare text entry carries `data-field-entry`, which the global rule skips, and the frame around the chips carries `data-field-frame`, which takes the outline and halo while the entry has keyboard focus. Every other interactive primitive keeps its visual size and reaches a 48px pointer target through the `hit-area` utility in `theme.css`: an invisible `::before` on the positioned control, grown to `--size-hit-target` around its centre on each axis where the control is smaller, without moving layout. The top bar pads its top, and the tab bar and the sheet their bottom, by the device's safe-area insets. The installed app's name and colours belong to production. Every routed page opens with `PortalPageHeader`, which owns the one `<h1>` and the 32px gap to the content below it; a page with a richer hero, such as the coach's client detail, keeps its own layout but takes the title's typography from `PORTAL_PAGE_TITLE_CLASS`. The builder canvases — the program editor and the nutrition-plan, nutrition-day and recipe builders — keep their own sticky chrome bar in place of a page header, because their toolbar is the page's heading row and has to stay with the canvas as it scrolls.

### Typography

The portals set type on a six-rung ladder: the page title in the serif face at regular weight; the hero value (`value-lg`, 24/32 sans medium with tight tracking and tabular numerals); the widget title (16px semibold, the only 600 weight inside a widget); body (14px regular, with medium for the one emphasized token in a row); the field label (`LABEL_CLASS`, 12px medium in secondary ink, sentence case) naming a field or a value, such as a builder setting (Weeks, Tempo, Last-set technique), a definition term (Age, Total, Next check-in form) or a stat's caption; and the eyebrow (`EYEBROW_CLASS`, the `label` step: 12px semibold uppercase at `tracking-label`) for what stands above or heads a section rather than naming a value: a page eyebrow, an overline section head such as Your order or Blocks, a card's kicker such as a session's days, the weekday on a day cell, the Superset and Soon tags, and the group heads of the editor's Checks (Before you can publish, Worth a look). `typography.ts` also names three screen styles off the ladder's rungs: `SUPPORTING_TEXT_CLASS` (14px secondary, a supporting line under a heading, such as the week card's dates, block line and status note), `CAPTION_CLASS` (12px secondary, a caption such as a field's hint, "A deload is always 1 week") and `SCREEN_CARD_TITLE_CLASS` (the serif face at 18px regular in primary ink, a builder session's name, collapsed or edited in place). The printed program keeps its own heads and eyebrows. Bold is reserved for the 10px `micro` marks. The serif face appears only on the page title, the brand word, a widget's voice line, a session's name on its card, and the printed program, whose title and section headings set it in semibold and whose focus line sets it in italic.

Inside the portals, interaction colour is `primary` and its derivatives, never `brand`: `primary` is the brand magenta on the client portal and near-black on the coach portal, and the focus ring follows it, so checked controls, active navigation, hovered triggers and keyboard focus agree on each side. `brand` is kept for the public site and brand identity marks. A pressed toggle fills with `primary-pressed` rather than `primary`: the brand magenta darkened to #9E1755 in the client portal, so its white 14px label reaches APCA Lc 90 where `primary` reaches 80.8, and the coach's own near-black `primary` in the coach portal. Attention markers such as unread dots and counters are `status-pending`. `AttentionDot` is the one 8px dot: the notification bell's unread marker and each unread notification, and the client navigation, which marks a link in the sidebar, the More sheet and on the More tab when the link lives in the sheet, while it holds something she has not opened, and speaks it as "(new)" after the link's name. Teal is shared by both portals and does not separate them.

A stat widget has one anatomy: a header of icon and title, where the title is the hero's label so no uppercase label sits under it; the hero, with an optional inline unit in 14px medium secondary on the same baseline; one 14px secondary context line directly under the hero; and a footer pinned to the card's bottom that holds navigation only. Management actions live in the header as ghost icon buttons with a spoken label, one per action and a menu only for two or more, and the client portal shows the same widget without them. An empty state keeps the card's shape: a muted placeholder in the hero slot, the explanation in the context line, and the one thing to do as a `sm` primary text button in the body. A widget in a narrow column, such as the client sidebar's next check-in, takes a compact density that scales the same anatomy down one step: a 14px semibold title with its icon, body-size content, 16px padding on the card corner with no shadow, one `sm` primary button at full width, and the navigation link centred beneath it as part of the same stacked group. A list widget that the viewer may add to, such as the client's measurements, carries a quiet add in the same header slot, a `sm` outline "Add" with a leading plus, since the header never holds a filled control; while the list is empty the header add gives way to a `sm` primary text button in the body that names what is added ("Add your first measurements"). A page has at most one primary action, so the profile's picture controls are small outline and ghost buttons beside the avatar, and the identity block sets the name on the hero rung with the email as its context line rather than under an eyebrow. That block is one composition, `AvatarField`, on her profile page, the coach's page of a client's profile and the coach's own profile: the lg avatar with its initials fallback, the name and the context line, a ghost `sm` Remove while a picture exists, confirmed through the destructive `ConfirmDialog`, and the `sm` outline `FilePickerButton` reading Upload picture or Change picture, which takes a JPEG, PNG or WebP up to the progress photo limit. A profile page edits in sections, each a settings section with its own footer save, and a value nobody may change, such as her email or her preferred check-in day, reads as plain text with a muted `IconHint` saying why. Secondary detail at the bottom of a page, such as the booking behind the coach's Assessment call widget and a client's onboarding once her answers are approved, takes the collapsible widget frame: the same panel and header of icon and title, where the `h2` title is an Accordion trigger stretched over the whole header with the chevron at its trailing edge, collapsed by default, and the body opens 16px under the header. The header takes the same `titleAdornment` beside the title and `action` before the chevron as `PortalWidget`, such as the onboarding widget's screening warning and status badge; both sit above the stretched trigger, so a click on them never toggles the widget.

A settings section is a panel with a header band carrying an icon and the widget-title rung plus an optional one-line description, hairline-separated rows each with a title, a short description and an example and no icon of their own, since the header's icon names the section, the control trailing the text for an instant preference or stacked under it for a form field; preferences apply on change, a form saves once from a footer button disabled until something changed, warnings read inline in the pending tone, and no page has a page-wide save. A settings page holds settings sections only, never a dashboard widget: the client's subscription is a section whose rows are the plan, whose description carries the payment date and whether it has started, started, or been cancelled, so it wears no status badge, the renewal or access date, and, while cancelling is possible, a cancel row whose description states the terms (the refund deadline, or that access stays to the period end with no refund) beside a red-ink outline button that opens the confirm dialog; the coach's client record keeps the read-only subscription summary widget.

An appointment is one card on both portals, the other party is its attendee (the client on the coach's pages, the coach on the client's), the type and the status are badges from the shared vocabulary, and the actions sit in the card's action slot, trailing on wide screens and stacked below on phones. The card's date sits on the body rung beside the name, and a join link on a list card is an outline button, since the page's one primary is the request action; only the sidebar's next check-in, whose sole purpose is joining, carries a solid join. In a dashboard list every row action is an outline, and at most one row per widget, the one that is live right now such as a call marked Today, carries the solid join. A check-in's status is always a badge beside its type, pending in colour when the viewer owes the reply and muted when the other party does, and the action slot shows only the action that matters now, with the rest behind a ghost `RowActionsMenu` at the row's end: Join Meet on an upcoming check-in with Reschedule and a destructive Cancel last in the menu, Decline and Approve (or Accept) side by side on a request waiting for the viewer with Reschedule in the menu, Cancel request alone on the viewer's own request, and nothing on a past one. Each menu item appears only while the domain allows it, the menu is left out when it would be empty, its icon trigger is named for the check-in's date and time and matches the row's button height (`RowActionsMenu` `size`: `sm`, 40px, by default; `xs`, 32px, in a builder row), and below `md` the shown actions share one row with the trigger rather than stacking; both portals' check-in pages share the frame of segmented tabs, a muted count line, and the card list at the portal column's full width.

- `DM Sans` is the body and interface family; `Playfair Display` is the heading and display family. Both are emitted at runtime as `--font-sans` and `--font-serif`, which code outside CSS (the generated resource page art) reads instead of naming a family.
- The core scale is the framework's `xs` to `5xl` steps plus `caption` (11px) for the smallest readable text — calendar weekdays, chart axes, phone-preview body copy — `md` (15px) between `sm` and `base`, `label` (12px semibold at `tracking-label`, the uppercase store tags and badges), and the display steps `display-sm` (24px), `display-md` (32px), and fluid `display-lg` (44–72px).
- Available weights are regular 400, medium 500, and semibold 600. Two more are reserved: light 300 for the hero's lead copy over video, and bold 700 for prices and the tracked uppercase `micro` labels. Line heights are the framework's `tight` (1.25, every heading that sets one), `snug`, `normal` and `relaxed`, plus `display-snug` (1.1) for large display headings and `heading` (1.2) inside the display steps.
- `tracking-label` and `tracking-section-eyebrow` are the letter-spacing roles; everything else uses the framework's `tracking-*` steps at their own values, `tracking-wide` (0.025em) for navigation links and short badges.
- The printed program carries its own scale, `document-title`, `document-chapter`, `document-figure`, `document-section`, `document-card`, `document-body`, `document-table` and `document-note`, each a multiple of one anchor, `--document-root`, that the document article sets: 1.125rem on screen (so table text is about 16.3px and notes 15.4px) and, in print, the page's own 1rem, which `print.css` sets to 10.5pt, where they land on 28, 19.5, 17.9, 16, 13, 10.5, 9.5 and 9pt (`document-title` is the cover's alone, `document-chapter` a block's name, `document-figure` a figure that stands alone, a day tile's date numeral and the week's daily steps); neighbouring levels step by at most about 1.5×; nested document text never compounds, and outside the article, such as in the coach's text editor, the anchor is 1rem. Three rungs ride on the note size: the document eyebrow, semibold uppercase at `tracking-label` in secondary ink, for the cover's eyebrow, a day tile's weekday (in the day's own ink), a day page's date on a phone, routine band titles and the "Daily steps" label; the document head, semibold primary ink in ordinary capitalisation, for table and log heads, the write-in labels and the note caption; and the document note, plain secondary ink for asides such as an exercise's rest and swap line. Section, card and session titles are serif semibold with lining figures; data values — dates, reps, set numbers, rest, minutes, steps — are bold sans with tabular figures. The meta line beside or under a heading, such as "From Coach Eli · published 16 November 2026", a block's dates and a week's "Hypertrophy · week 1 of 6", is plain secondary ink on the table size. The shared classes live in `typography.ts`: `DOCUMENT_TITLE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_CHAPTER_TITLE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_SECTION_TITLE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_CARD_TITLE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_EYEBROW_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_PROSE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_VALUE_CLASS` (semibold tabular primary figures inside text), `DOCUMENT_FIGURE_CLASS` (a figure on the `document-figure` rung), `DOCUMENT_HEAD_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_TERM_CLASS` (a term's label: body size, semibold primary), `DOCUMENT_META_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_NOTE_CLASS`, `DOCUMENT_MARK_CLASS` (a round mark 1.9em of the note size, shared by `SetMark` and `PositionBadge` at `document`) and `DOCUMENT_RULE_CLASS` (a heading's 2px bottom rule, `document-tight` above it, coloured by the caller), so the coach's text editor sets her texts in the same type as the print. ### Spacing

The spacing scale is `space-0` (0), `space-1` (4px), `space-2` (8px), `space-3` (10px), `space-4` (12px), `space-5` (16px), `space-6` (24px), `space-7` (32px), `space-8` (48px), `space-9` (64px), and `space-10` (96px).

- `space-1` to `space-2` handle icon gaps and other micro-spacing.
- `space-3` to `space-5` handle compact control padding and tightly related content.
- `space-6` to `space-7` handle card padding and component groups.
- `space-8` to `space-10` handle major layout and section separation.
- `document-tight`, `document-paragraph`, `document-gap` and `document-section` are the printed program's rhythm, multiples of `--document-root` declared beside the type stops (0.5714, 0.75, 1.1429 and 2.2857 of it), so they scale with the sheet like the type and land on 6, 8, 12 and 24pt in print: between a heading and its text, between paragraphs of prose, between the blocks of a section, and between sections. `document-row` is a printed table row, a set row, a segment row and a segment heading's minimum: 44px on screen, the viewer's row, and 8mm in print, a handwriting line. `leading-document-prose` (1.55) is the prose leading. Three document geometry variables sit beside the anchor: `--document-term-column` (10em) and `--document-term-gap` (1.4em), every term grid's label column and gap in ems of its own text, and `--week-column-gap` (3mm), the gap between a week's seven columns, from which a day page's tile column is computed.
